## Checks run

- **AI-chat links / pasted-chat markup in the current live bodies:** none in any of the 10 articles, or in the redirected `iodine-and-your-hair` post folded into the iodine merge target.
- **Citation style:** every draft has exactly 3 distinct PubMed studies, each linked once, first link at 58–65% of the text. Key Takeaways and FAQ carry no links.
- **Claims scan:** no cure, prevent, reverse, heal or guarantee language. The only hits are neutral wording ("treated" in lab methods, "Treat licorice products as unproven", "medical treatment" in an adenosine FAQ heading).
- **Builder checks:** all PMIDs verified through `pubmed.ncbi`; product, testimonial and footer blocks match the live articles; no stray `**`; no reviewer line.

## Flags that need your decision

- **Thin or no hair evidence (honest, shorter articles):** `monolaurin`, `inulin`, `lecithin`, `hesperidin` (one lab study), `oyster-extract` (zinc evidence only), `licorice-root`. You may prefer to hold or merge some of these.
- **Strongest evidence this batch:** `adenosine` (two randomized trials in men plus a 2025 review, still graded low overall) and `silica` (two placebo-controlled trials of one specific form, ch-OSA).
- **Apple stem cell extract:** the hair trials are on apple procyanidin B2 and an Annurca apple supplement, not on "stem cell extract". The article says so. If FullyVital products use stem cell extract, the evidence does not transfer. Kamimura 2000 called procyanidin B-2 a "promising cure"; the article reports only the hair counts.
- **Disease research mentioned neutrally:** androgenetic alopecia (adenosine trials), thyroid disease as a cause of shedding (iodine), atopic dermatitis as the source of bacteria (monolaurin), blood pressure effects of licorice.
- **Closing product box** still says products "Promote Hair Regrowth". Unchanged and flagged on every draft.
- **Soft statements to check:** licorice FAQ "In small amounts occasionally, probably", inulin "Adding fiber gradually is the usual approach", oyster extract shellfish-allergy caution (general advice, not from an abstract).
