## Flags that need your decision

- **DMDM hydantoin (compliance):** the old post asserted that DMDM hydantoin causes hair loss and offered ways to "combat" it. No source supports that. The rewrite says no study links it to hair loss and covers the skin-allergy research instead. One of the three sources (Uter 2002) has no abstract in PubMed, so the article reports only its title and scope and says so.
- **Iron deficiency:** the evidence conflicts (a 36-study meta-analysis found lower ferritin in women with hair loss; a controlled clinic study found no excess of iron deficiency). The article presents both and sends readers to a doctor for testing, with no supplement advice.
- **Medical procedures:** `hyaluronic-acid` (injections and a filler case report) and `microneedling-hairline` (the trials all used minoxidil as the base; no hairline-specific study exists). Both are described neutrally with a doctor/practitioner referral. The microneedling article does not mention FullyVital's derma roller.
- **Medical mentions (neutral):** PCOS (inositol studies), dandruff and seborrheic dermatitis (scalp detox).
- **No hair evidence (honest, shorter):** `tulsi` (stress and general trials only; hair cortisol is a stress marker, not hair growth), `inositol` (a rat study of a combination plus two PCOS studies on acne and excess hair), `olive-oil` (mouse study of oleuropein; a forearm study where olive oil weakened the skin barrier), `scalp-detox-treatment` ("detox" is not a researched concept; the article covers dandruff and microbiome research instead).
- **Strongest hair evidence:** `microneedling-hairline`, `caffeine` (systematic review plus a double-blind shampoo trial), `iron-deficiency-hair-loss`.
- **General advice not from the studies:** a few lines are labelled or phrased as general care (patch test, be gentle with exfoliation); see each `review.md` for the flagged lines.
