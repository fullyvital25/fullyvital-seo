What Is A Hair Whorl?

A hair whorl (or crown) is the point where hairs spiral outward in a clockwise or counterclockwise direction. A cowlick is a whorl located along the front hairline.

Does Your Whorl Affect Hair Growth?

Not as far as the research shows. A whorl is about the direction hair lies, not how fast it grows. If you want to know whether hair is growing normally, track length and density over time, not whorl position.

Styling With A Whorl

  • Work with the direction. Hair tends to follow the whorl, so cut and part with it in mind.
  • Ask your stylist to look at your crown before choosing a cut.
  • Don't confuse a whorl with thinning. A swirl of hair at the crown that shows scalp may be your whorl, but check with a dermatologist if the area is widening.

What The Research Shows

Normal Patterns

A review of scalp whorls reports that single and double parietal whorls are examples of normal scalp patterns. Frontal patterns are variable, and a whorl with a cowlick can be seen at any location along the frontal hairline. The review also noted that abnormally placed or absent scalp whorls have been associated with abnormal brain development, while describing cases of unusual whorls in people with normal development (Samlaska et al., 1989).

Are Whorls Genetic?

In a study of 37 pairs of twins and children from the Northern and Southern hemispheres, twins' whorls rotated preferentially in the same direction, which the authors took as evidence that whorl formation is a genetically determined developmental process. Counterclockwise whorls were more frequent in the Southern hemisphere sample (Willems et al., 2024).

Differences Between Populations

A study of Japanese individuals found whorl orientation to be random, leading the author to conclude it isn't genetically determined in that population, and that genetic variation for the trait exists between regions (Klar, 2009).

Frequently Asked Questions

Does a hair whorl affect hair growth?

We found no study showing that it does. A whorl affects the direction hair lies.

Is a hair whorl genetic?

Twin data suggest genetic influence, but a study of Japanese individuals found random orientation, so the picture isn't settled.

Is a double whorl normal?

Yes. Single and double parietal whorls are normal scalp patterns. If you have a concern about an unusual whorl, ask your doctor.
