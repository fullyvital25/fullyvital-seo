What Is Vitamin K?

Vitamin K comes in forms called K1 (phylloquinone) and K2 (menaquinones). In a Polish study, K1 came mainly from vegetables and K2 mainly from processed meat and high-fat cheese and cream.

Does Vitamin K Help Hair?

Not as far as we can find. Several nutritional deficiencies overlap in how they look on the skin and hair, so a doctor is the right person to sort out the cause of hair loss.

If You Take Warfarin

Do not start vitamin K supplements without talking to your doctor. Warfarin works against vitamin K, so changes in intake can affect your anticoagulation.

What The Research Shows

Food Sources And Warfarin

Dark green vegetables are the primary dietary sources of vitamin K, and it's also found in certain plant oils and prepared foods that contain them, such as baked goods, margarines and salad dressings. Patients on warfarin are typically told to consume a constant dietary vitamin K intake that meets dietary recommendations of 65 to 80 micrograms a day (Booth & Centurelli, 1999).

Where People Get It

In the PURE Poland study of 1,985 participants, total vitamin K intake averaged 331 µg a day in men and 359 in women. Vitamin K1 came mainly from vegetables (56.5% to 76.8%), K2 mainly from processed meat and high-fat cheese and cream (70.1% to 77.6%) (Regulska-Ilow et al., 2022).

Deficiency And The Skin

A review of skin signs of nutritional disorders found that hair loss with a rash around the body openings, typical of zinc deficiency, can also be seen with essential fatty acid deficiency, biotinidase deficiency and kwashiorkor. Because signs overlap between deficiencies, a clinician is needed to tell them apart (Prendiville & Manfredi, 1992). The review didn't list vitamin K among them.
