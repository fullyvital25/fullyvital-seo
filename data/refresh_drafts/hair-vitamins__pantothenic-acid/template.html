Pantothenic acid (vitamin B5) is essential, widely available in food, and almost never deficient. The hair evidence is early: lab work on hair follicle cells from minks, and an open-label study of a combination supplement that included pantothenic acid, which found less hair loss over 12 weeks.

Here's what those studies show, and what they can't tell you about vitamin B5 alone.

Key Takeaways

  • Pantothenic acid is a water-soluble vitamin that is a component of coenzyme A. It is widely distributed in animal and plant foods, deficiency is rare and toxicity is negligible.
  • In cultured mink hair follicles and dermal papilla cells, pantothenic acid promoted follicle growth and cell proliferation.
  • In a 12-week open-label study of 112 people with light diffuse hair loss, a supplement containing millet extract, pantothenic acid, L-cystine and biotin was linked to reduced hair loss. It had no control group and many ingredients.
  • We found no trial of pantothenic acid on its own for hair.

What Is Pantothenic Acid?

Pantothenic acid, also called vitamin B5, is a water-soluble vitamin. Your body uses it as a component of coenzyme A and acyl carrier protein.

Does Vitamin B5 Help Hair?

It might in the lab, but not shown in people on its own. The idea that pantothenic acid helps hair is common. The real evidence is a cell and follicle study in animals and a combination supplement study.

Getting Enough Pantothenic Acid

  • Food first. It is found in both animal and plant foods, so a varied diet usually covers it.
  • Don't expect hair benefits from megadoses. There's no such evidence.
  • Ask your doctor about supplements if you're pregnant, breastfeeding, have a condition or take medication.

What The Research Shows

Intake, Deficiency And Safety

A scoping review for the Nordic Nutrition Recommendations describes pantothenic acid as an essential component of coenzyme A and acyl carrier protein, widely distributed in animal- and plant-source foods. Deficiency is rare and toxicity negligible, and no health concerns related to pantothenic acid were identified (Freese et al., 2023).

Hair Follicle Cells In The Lab

In cultured mink hair follicles and dermal papilla cells, pantothenic acid at 20 and 40 μg/ml promoted hair follicle growth and the expression of IGF-1 and VEGF in dermal papilla cells. The effect was mediated through the ID3/Notch signalling pathway (Wang et al., 2020). This was an animal cell study.

A Combination Supplement

In an exploratory open-label study of 112 mostly female adults with light diffuse hair loss, a supplement containing millet extract, pantothenic acid, L-cystine and biotin was taken for 12 weeks. Hair density and the proportion of anagen hairs increased, and hair loss declined by 59%. It was well tolerated. With no control group and four active ingredients, it can't show what pantothenic acid did (Proksch & Bartylla, 2026).

Frequently Asked Questions

Does pantothenic acid help hair growth?

In mink follicle cells in the lab it promoted growth. In people, a combination supplement with it was linked to less hair loss in an uncontrolled study, but pantothenic acid alone hasn't been tested.

Is vitamin B5 deficiency common?

No. Deficiency is rare.

Is vitamin B5 safe?

Toxicity is negligible according to a Nordic nutrition review. Talk to your doctor before taking supplements.
