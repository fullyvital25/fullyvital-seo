What The Research Shows

Copper Powers The Pigment Enzyme

Copper acts as a cofactor for tyrosinase, the key enzyme in pigmentation (Tsang et al., 2021). Tyrosinase starts the chain of reactions that turns the amino acid tyrosine into melanin, the pigment that gives your hair its color.

Low Copper In Premature Graying

In 66 young people under 20 with premature graying, blood copper was significantly lower than in matched controls (Fatemi Naieni et al., 2012). This shows an association, not cause and effect. But it makes copper one of the most relevant minerals to check if you're graying early.

Copper Peptides In The Lab

A copper peptide, AHK-Cu, was tested directly on human hair follicles. It lengthened hair follicles kept alive outside the body, stimulated the growth of dermal papilla cells (the cells that direct hair growth), and shifted markers away from cell death (Pyo et al., 2007). These are laboratory results, and clinical trials in people are still needed.

Can You Have Too Much Copper?

Yes. Stick to recommended amounts. If you have a condition that affects copper metabolism, take copper supplements only under medical supervision.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does copper help gray hair?

Copper is required for the pigment enzyme tyrosinase, and young people who gray prematurely tended to have lower copper levels. Keeping your copper intake adequate supports normal pigment production.

Do copper peptides help hair growth?

In laboratory studies, the copper peptide AHK-Cu lengthened human hair follicles and boosted dermal papilla cell growth. Human clinical trials are still needed.

Can copper deficiency cause hair loss or color loss?

Deficiency is uncommon in adults eating a varied diet. If you're concerned about your copper levels, ask your doctor about a blood test.
