Who Should Choose Which?

  • You have a diagnosed biotin deficiency, or a doctor has recommended biotin: biotin is the one with a clear role.
  • You're pregnant or planning to be: ask your doctor about a prenatal vitamin.
  • You're just trying to improve thinning hair: get tested first. Ask your doctor about iron, vitamin D and other markers rather than guessing.

What Does Biotin Do For Hair?

Biotin is a water-soluble vitamin that acts as a cofactor for four carboxylase enzymes. In infants, children and adults, deficiency causes alopecia and a characteristic scaly, red rash around body openings (Mock, 1991).

In a review of 18 reported cases of biotin use for hair and nail changes, every patient had an underlying cause for poor hair or nail growth, and all improved after biotin. The reviewers concluded that biotin may help in acquired or inherited biotin deficiency and in conditions like brittle nails or uncombable hair, but that these cases are uncommon and there is a lack of sufficient evidence for supplementation in healthy people (Patel et al., 2017).

A later review found only three studies of oral biotin for hair growth or quality. The best, a double-blind placebo-controlled study, found no difference between biotin and placebo. The other two were in specific groups (patients on isotretinoin and women after sleeve gastrectomy) and had a risk of bias (Yelich et al., 2024).

Is It Safe?
