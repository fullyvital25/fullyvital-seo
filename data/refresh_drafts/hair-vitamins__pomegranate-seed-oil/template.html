What Is Pomegranate Seed Oil?

Pomegranate seed oil is pressed from the seeds of Punica granatum. Its main fatty acid is punicic acid.

What The Research Shows

Plant Oils On Skin

A review covering topical plant oils, including pomegranate seed oil, looked at their anti-inflammatory and antioxidant effects on skin, wound healing and repair of the skin barrier. It was about skin, not the scalp or hair (Lin et al., 2017).

Skin Cells In The Lab

A lab study tested oils and fatty acids on human keratinocytes and fibroblasts. Oils rich in linoleic acid promoted proliferation, while punicic acid and pomegranate seed oil had a significant negative impact. The oils did not meaningfully change the closure rate of an artificial wound gap (Poljšak et al., 2025). The authors framed this as relevant to skin repair, and it was in vitro.

Pomegranate Leaf In Mice

Researchers applied alcoholic and aqueous extracts of pomegranate leaves to shaved mice. Transition from rest to growth (telogen to anagen) was seen in about 45% and 27% of animals with the two extracts, against 51% with minoxidil. The alcoholic extract also showed antidandruff activity against fungal strains in lab tests (Bhinge et al., 2021). This was leaf extract in mice, not seed oil in people.
