Here's what that study found, what the skin research shows, and what to know about using Centella.

What Is Centella Asiatica?

Centella asiatica, also known as gotu kola, is a medicinal herb. Its triterpenes include asiaticoside, madecassoside, asiatic acid and madecassic acid.

What The Research Shows

A Hair Cell Study

Researchers tested a titrated extract of Centella asiatica on 3D spheroid cultures of human dermal papilla cells. It increased cell viability and spheroid size only in 3D cultures, reduced STAT signalling, and increased the expression of hair-growth-related signature genes, suggesting it activates the hair-inductive capacity of these cells (Choi et al., 2017). This was a cell study.

The Skin Research

A review of Centella and skin diseases reports that the herb and its triterpenes exert therapeutic effects in conditions such as acne, burns, atopic dermatitis and wounds through several signalling pathways in lab and animal studies, and says rigorously controlled long-term clinical trials are needed (Park, 2021).

A Cream With Centella

In 88 Chinese women with sensitive skin, a facial cream containing Centella asiatica leaf extract, ceramide NP and panthenol was applied for 4 weeks. Sensitive-skin scores fell by 66% at 2 weeks and 76% at 4 weeks, and all adverse events were mild and self-limiting (Su et al., 2025). It was a facial study of a combination product.
