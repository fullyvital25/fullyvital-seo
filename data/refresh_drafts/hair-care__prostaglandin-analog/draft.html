What Are Prostaglandin Analogs?

Prostaglandin analogs are synthetic compounds that mimic natural prostaglandins. They are used in glaucoma treatment, and bimatoprost 0.03% is FDA-approved for eyelash hypotrichosis.

What The Research Shows

Trials Of Topical Prostaglandin Analogs

A systematic review and meta-analysis of six randomized trials, all comparing prostaglandin analogs with placebo, in hair, eyelashes and eyebrows, found they significantly improved hair length and density (p < 0.001). There was no significant difference in adverse events. The authors said the best dose and frequency need further study (Jiang et al., 2023).

Prostaglandin D2: The Opposite Effect

Prostaglandin D2 (PGD2) is elevated in bald scalp compared with haired scalp in men with androgenetic alopecia. PGD2 inhibited hair growth in explanted human follicles and when applied to mice, and the effect required the receptor GPR44. The authors suggested the PGD2-GPR44 pathway as a potential treatment target (Garza et al., 2012).

Safety Questions

Bimatoprost 0.03% was approved by the FDA for eyelash hypotrichosis in 2008. A review found the literature is dominated by industry-sponsored trials, and that study design choices may have systematically underreported application discomfort and prostaglandin-associated periorbitopathy. The risk of increased iris pigmentation remains inadequately investigated, so patients need education and monitoring (Steinsapir & Steinsapir, 2021).
