What Is Borage Seed Oil?

Borage oil comes from the seeds of Borago officinalis. It's a source of gamma-linolenic acid (GLA) and is used in pharmaceutical and health food products.

Does Borage Oil Help Hair?

There's no evidence for hair. The research is on skin barrier function, and the hair claims you may see are not backed by the studies we found.

What The Research Shows

Skin Barrier In Elderly People

Twenty-nine healthy elderly people took borage oil capsules providing 360 or 720 mg GLA a day for 2 months. Transepidermal water loss decreased by a mean of 10.8%, itch complaints fell from 34% to 0%, and GLA in red cell membranes rose 70%. Skin hydration did not change significantly. The abstract describes no placebo group (Brosche & Platt, 2000).

Cochrane Review On Eczema

A Cochrane review of 27 studies (8 of borage oil) found that borage oil failed to significantly improve global eczema symptoms compared with placebo. Adverse effects were mild, transient and mainly gastrointestinal, and long-term effects were not examined (Bamford et al., 2013).

Pyrrolizidine Alkaloids

Pyrrolizidine alkaloids are known for hepatotoxicity, so there is a health risk with medicinal herbs that contain them. There is no international regulation of PAs in foods, though for herbal preparations the limit is 1 µg/kg. Borage seed oil is a GLA source with growing use, and a mass spectrometry method was developed to check commercial borage seed oil for PAs (Vacillotto et al., 2013).

Evening primrose oil is another oil people compare it with; see evening primrose oil and hair.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does borage oil help hair growth?

We found no study showing that it does.

Is borage oil safe?

Borage plants can contain liver-toxic pyrrolizidine alkaloids, so choose products tested for them, and talk to your doctor first.

Does borage oil help skin?

In a small study of elderly people it reduced water loss through the skin, but a Cochrane review found no benefit for eczema.
