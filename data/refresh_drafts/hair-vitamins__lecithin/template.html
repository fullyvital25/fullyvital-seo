Lecithin is a mix of fatty substances called phospholipids, used in foods, supplements and cosmetics, where it works as an emulsifier that helps ingredients blend. We found no study showing that eating or applying lecithin grows hair. Its best-documented hair role is as a helper that makes other ingredients work better.

Here's what is known about lecithin in cosmetics, in formulas, and in the diet.

Key Takeaways

  • Lecithin and related phosphoglycerides are used in cosmetics as skin and hair conditioning agents, emulsifiers and surfactants, and an expert panel found them safe at current use levels.
  • The same safety review notes that phospholipids' effects in the body are not reproduced by applying them to the skin.
  • In a rat study, lecithin was part of a delivery system that took melatonin into hair follicles. The hair growth effect was attributed to the delivery formula.
  • We found no trial of lecithin supplements or lecithin hair treatments for hair growth in people.

Does Lecithin Do Anything For Hair?

Lecithin shows up in hair-care products mostly as a conditioner and emulsifier, which makes oil and water ingredients blend. That's a product-texture job. For hair growth, the honest answer is that it hasn't been tested.

How To Think About Lecithin

  • As a conditioner: it's a safe, common cosmetic ingredient, and it can make a product feel better on hair.
  • As a hair-growth ingredient: not supported by studies we found.

What The Research Shows

Cosmetic Safety

The Cosmetic Ingredient Review panel assessed lecithin and 16 other phosphoglycerides. They function mainly as skin and hair conditioning agents, emulsifying agents and surfactants, and they're used at up to 50% concentrations. The panel concluded they are safe in the present practices of use and concentration. It also noted that although phospholipids have physiologic effects, these are not reproduced by applying phospholipid ingredients to the skin (Johnson et al., 2020).

Lecithin As A Delivery Helper

Researchers loaded melatonin into a lecithin-enhanced Pickering emulsion and tested it in a rat model of testosterone-induced androgenic alopecia. The formula improved melatonin's follicular delivery and photostability, prolonged the anagen (growth) phase and increased follicular density (Elshall et al., 2023). This was an animal study of a delivery formula, and the effect is credited to melatonin, with lecithin helping it reach the follicle.

Lecithin And Choline

In a small trial of 12 healthy men, a soy-derived lysophosphatidylcholine, a choline-containing phospholipid found in foods, raised plasma choline, so it can be a source of choline (Tanaka-Kanegae et al., 2024). That study didn't look at hair.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can lecithin help with hair loss?

We found no study showing it can. Lecithin is used in cosmetics as a conditioner and emulsifier.

Does lecithin improve hair shine?

As a conditioning agent in products it's used for hair, but we found no study measuring shine.

Is lecithin safe?

A cosmetic safety panel found lecithin and related phosphoglycerides safe at present use levels. Talk to your doctor before taking supplements if you're pregnant, breastfeeding, have a condition or take medication.
