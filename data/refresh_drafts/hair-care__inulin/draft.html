What Is Inulin?

Inulin is a soluble dietary fiber stored by plants as a reserve carbohydrate. It's used in the food industry and sold as a supplement.

Does Inulin Do Anything For Hair?

The claims you see online that inulin "nourishes the scalp" or "stimulates follicles" aren't backed by studies we could find. Any hair benefit would be indirect, through gut or mineral effects, and no research has connected those to hair.

What The Research Shows

Sources And Roles

Inulin is found in more than 36,000 plant species. The main raw materials for industry are Jerusalem artichoke and chicory root, and it's also in onion, garlic and barley. As a prebiotic it supports beneficial gut bacteria, and reviewed benefits include lipid metabolism, blood sugar, mineral absorption and constipation (Qin et al., 2023).

What Human Trials Show

A systematic review of human trials in healthy adults found inulin-type fructans promote Bifidobacterium, Lactobacillus and Faecalibacterium prausnitzii, and reported better laxation, improved lipid profile and increased absorption of calcium and magnesium. It noted that evidence on chain length is limited (Hughes et al., 2022).

Calcium Absorption In Adolescents

In a randomized trial, pubertal adolescents took 8 g a day of an inulin-type fructan or a placebo. Calcium absorption was significantly higher in the fructan group at 8 weeks (8.5 percentage points) and at 1 year (5.9), and bone mineral content and density gained more (Abrams et al., 2005).

Frequently Asked Questions

Does inulin help hair growth?

We found no study showing it does. The claims are not backed by research.

Is inulin safe?

It's a common dietary fiber. Talk to your doctor before taking it as a supplement if you're pregnant, breastfeeding, have a digestive condition or take medication.

What foods contain inulin?

Chicory root, Jerusalem artichoke, onion, garlic and barley.
