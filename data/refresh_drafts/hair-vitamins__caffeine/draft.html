What The Research Shows

Systematic Review

A systematic review of topical caffeine for hair loss searched to November 2024 and included 9 trials with 684 people. All conclusions favoured topical caffeine, but the quality of evidence was medium in 3 studies, low in 1 and very low in the other 5. The main flaws were no randomization, placebo or control groups and missing information on caffeine concentration. The authors said better-designed trials of well-defined products are needed, and that satisfactory outcomes are not guaranteed (Szendzielorz and Spiewak, 2025).

A Shampoo Trial In Women

In a single-center, double-blind trial, women with androgenetic alopecia were randomized to a phyto-caffeine shampoo or a control shampoo for 6 months. The caffeine group had significantly fewer hairs pulled in a hair pull test (-3.1 against -0.5, P < 0.001), and most secondary measures also improved. Both shampoos were very well tolerated (Bussoletti et al., 2020). The product was a specific branded formulation.

Does It Reach The Follicle?

Researchers measured caffeine penetration from a shampoo applied for 2 minutes and found it penetrated via the hair follicles and the stratum corneum. Penetration via follicles was faster and higher, and follicles were the only pathway for fast absorption during the first 20 minutes (Otberg et al., 2007). That study measured absorption, not hair growth.
