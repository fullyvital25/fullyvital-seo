Key Takeaways

  • A systematic review of 22 clinical studies (1,127 people) found microneedling improved hair measures as an add-on in androgenetic alopecia and alopecia areata, with no serious adverse events among 657 people. The authors rated the data as low quality.
  • In a pilot of 100 people with mild to moderate androgenetic alopecia, weekly microneedling plus 5% minoxidil gave a bigger hair count increase at 12 weeks than minoxidil alone (91.4 against 22.2).
  • In 60 people, 0.6 mm needles with minoxidil did better than minoxidil alone on hair count and thickness, and appeared more beneficial than 1.2 mm.
  • Studies used microneedling as an add-on. We found no study specific to hairlines.

What Is Microneedling?

Microneedling uses a device with fine needles, such as a dermaroller, to create controlled micro-injuries in the skin. In hair research it is done on the scalp at needle depths from about 0.5 to 2.5 mm.

Does It Help The Hairline?

Not shown for the hairline specifically. The trials treated the scalp in pattern hair loss, and results were as an add-on to minoxidil.

What The Research Shows

Pilot Randomized Study

In a randomized, evaluator-blinded pilot, 100 people with mild to moderate androgenetic alopecia received either weekly microneedling with twice-daily 5% minoxidil or minoxidil alone for 12 weeks. The mean change in hair count was 91.4 with microneedling and minoxidil against 22.2 with minoxidil, and 82% of patients in the microneedling group reported more than 50% improvement against 4.5% with minoxidil alone (Dhurat et al., 2013).

Systematic Review

A systematic review found 22 clinical studies with 1,127 participants. Microneedling improved hair parameters as an adjunct across genders, hair loss types, needle depths of 0.50 to 2.50 mm and session frequencies from weekly to monthly. No serious adverse events were reported across 657 people receiving it. The authors described the data as relatively low quality, with large differences between studies, and recommended large randomized trials (English et al., 2022).

Needle Depth

Sixty people with moderate to severe androgenetic alopecia were allocated to minoxidil alone, or minoxidil with biweekly microneedling at 1.2 mm or 0.6 mm for 12 weeks. All groups improved, and the 0.6 mm group had a greater rise in hair count and thickness than the minoxidil-only group. The authors said that depth tended to be more beneficial than 1.2 mm (Faghihi et al., 2021).

Frequently Asked Questions

Does microneedling regrow a receding hairline?

We found no hairline-specific study. Trials were in androgenetic alopecia of the scalp, as an add-on to minoxidil.

Is microneedling safe?

The systematic review found no serious adverse events, but data were low quality. Ask a qualified practitioner.

How often should I do it?

Studies used weekly to monthly sessions, with different depths. A doctor can advise.
