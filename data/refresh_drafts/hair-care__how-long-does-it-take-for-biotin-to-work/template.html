People ask how long biotin takes to work for hair, but the research doesn't support a timeline, because it doesn't show that biotin works for hair in people who aren't deficient. Reviews found limited evidence, and a lab-testing guideline warns that high-dose biotin can distort some blood tests.

Key Takeaways

  • We found no study that establishes how long biotin takes to affect hair in healthy people.
  • A review of the literature found 18 case reports of biotin for hair and nail changes, all in people with an underlying cause of poor hair or nail growth. All showed improvement. The authors found a lack of sufficient evidence for supplementation in healthy individuals.
  • A second review found only three eligible studies. The highest-quality one, double-blind and placebo-controlled, found no difference between biotin and placebo for hair growth.
  • A laboratory guidance document says high-dose biotin supplements can cause clinically significant errors in some blood tests.

What Is Biotin?

Biotin is a B vitamin, also called vitamin B7. It is popular as a hair and nail supplement.

So How Long Does It Take?

There is no reliable answer from the research. In the case reports, people had an underlying reason for poor hair growth, and the reviews didn't establish a timeline for others.

What To Do Instead

  • Ask your doctor why your hair is changing. Causes include nutrition and health conditions.
  • Tell your doctor and lab you take biotin before blood tests, since it can affect some results.
  • Don't expect quick results from any supplement.

What The Research Shows

Case Reports And Deficiency

A review searched for reports of biotin for hair loss and found 18 cases. In every case, patients had an underlying pathology for poor hair or nail growth, and all showed clinical improvement after biotin. The authors concluded that research demonstrating biotin's efficacy is limited, that supplementation may benefit people with acquired or inherited biotin deficiency, brittle nail syndrome or uncombable hair, and that there is a lack of sufficient evidence for healthy individuals (Patel et al., 2017).

Controlled Studies

A later review of oral biotin for hair growth or quality found only three studies. The first was double-blind and placebo-controlled and found no difference between biotin and placebo for hair growth. The other two studied specific groups (people on isotretinoin and women after sleeve gastrectomy) and were susceptible to bias, with no striking results. The authors said the utility of biotin as a hair supplement is not supported by high-quality studies (Yelich et al., 2024).

Lab Test Interference

A guidance document from the AACC explains that high circulating biotin can produce positive and negative interference in certain immunoassay blood tests. Case reports and in vivo studies show supplemental biotin can cause clinically significant errors in select tests. It notes that biotin is eliminated relatively quickly, so a repeat sample can be taken if interference is suspected (Li et al., 2020).

Frequently Asked Questions

How long does biotin take to work for hair?

The research doesn't give a timeline, and it doesn't show benefit in healthy people.

Does biotin affect lab tests?

Yes. High-dose biotin can interfere with some blood tests, so tell your doctor you take it.

Who might need biotin?

Reviews say people with biotin deficiency or certain conditions may benefit. A doctor can determine that.
