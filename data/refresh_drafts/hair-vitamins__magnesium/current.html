What Is Magnesium?

Magnesium is the fourth most abundant cation in the human body. It's involved in energy metabolism, nerve and muscle function, cardiovascular health, bone integrity and immune defense.

Does Magnesium Help Hair?

Not shown. Low magnesium is common, so some people may be short, but whether correcting it changes hair hasn't been demonstrated. The only hair-outcome trial we found had a null result for alopecia.

What The Research Shows

A Trial With A Hair Outcome

Sixty-four women with PCOS were randomized to magnesium or placebo for 10 weeks. Magnesium significantly improved quality of life, including physical functioning, emotional well-being and energy, compared with placebo. No significant effect was observed on acne, alopecia or abnormal uterine bleeding (Jaripur et al., 2022).

Magnesium Status

A review describes magnesium as a critical cofactor for hundreds of enzymes. It reports that deficiency is common worldwide, driven by inadequate dietary intake, chronic diseases, medication use and lifestyle, and that low magnesium status is associated with hypertension, type 2 diabetes, osteoporosis, migraines, depression and chronic inflammation (Matek Sarić et al., 2025).

How Much Supplemental Magnesium Is Tolerable

In 1997 the US Institute of Medicine set a tolerable upper intake level of 350 mg/day for adults from supplements alone, with diarrhea as the limiting factor. A 2023 perspective argued the level should be re-evaluated because seven studies using 128 to 1,200 mg/day found no significant difference in diarrhea between intervention and control groups (Costello et al., 2023).

Frequently Asked Questions

Does magnesium help hair growth?

We found no trial showing it does. In women with PCOS, 10 weeks of magnesium did not significantly change alopecia scores.

Can low magnesium cause hair loss?

We found no study showing that. Magnesium deficiency is common, and a doctor can check your levels if you're concerned.

How much magnesium can I take?

The US upper limit for supplements is 350 mg a day for adults, based on diarrhea. Talk to your doctor before taking more.
