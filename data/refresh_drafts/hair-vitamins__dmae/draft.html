What Is DMAE?

DMAE is 2-dimethylaminoethanol, also called deanol. It's used in topical anti-wrinkle products and has been sold in supplements.

What The Research Shows

Facial Skin Trial

A review describes DMAE as an analog of choline and a precursor of acetylcholine. In a randomized study, a 3% DMAE facial gel applied daily for 16 weeks was safe and improved forehead lines and periorbital fine wrinkles, with no differences in erythema, peeling, dryness, itching, burning or stinging versus placebo. An open-label extension to one year showed good safety (Grossman, 2005).

How It May Work

In cultured rabbit fibroblasts, 2.5 to 10 mmol/L DMAE caused massive vacuolization within hours. At 10 mmol/L it was moderately cytotoxic, and at 2.5 mmol/L it caused mitotic arrest. Topical 3% DMAE thickened rabbit ear epidermis. The authors suggested the vacuolar cytopathology may be the cellular basis of its antiwrinkle effect (Morissette et al., 2007).

Regulatory Status

A review of "nootropic" supplements noted that many popular nootropic ingredients, including dimethylaminoethanol, are unauthorized food or dietary supplement ingredients according to the European Commission (Jędrejko et al., 2023).

Frequently Asked Questions

Does DMAE help hair growth?

We found no study showing that it does. The research is on facial skin.

Is DMAE safe?

In a trial of 3% facial gel, application was well tolerated with a good safety profile up to a year. In cell studies it was cytotoxic at higher concentrations. Talk to your doctor first.

Is DMAE allowed in supplements?

The European Commission lists it as an unauthorized food or supplement ingredient.
