Key Takeaways

  • Female pattern hair loss typically presents as diffuse thinning over the crown, seen as an increased part width, with the frontal hairline spared.
  • In men, pattern hair loss typically shows as hairline recession and vertex balding.
  • Hair loss can be diffuse (shedding), patterned or focal, and a doctor can sort out which with a history, exam and targeted tests.
  • A part that stays the same width over months is not the same as one that is widening.

Normal Part Or Thinning?

A part is a gap in the hair, and its width varies with how you comb it and how thick your hair is. The more useful question is whether it is changing. Take photos of your part in the same light every few weeks. A part that gradually widens over months deserves attention.

Signs That Point To Thinning

  • A part that is getting wider, especially over the crown.
  • Hair that looks thinner overall, not just at the part.
  • Increased shedding that continues for weeks.

What The Research Shows

Patterns Of Hair Loss

Hair loss in men typically shows a distinctive pattern of hairline recession and vertex balding (Norwood-Hamilton classification), while women typically show diffuse thinning over the crown, seen as increased part width, with sparing of the frontal hairline (Ludwig classification). The review also noted that treatment aims to slow or stop progression, and that expectations need managing (Levy & Emer, 2013).

Diffuse, Patterned Or Focal

Nonscarring alopecias are categorized as diffuse, patterned or focal. Diffuse types such as telogen effluvium are usually self-limited and depend on stopping the underlying cause, for example stress. Patterned hair loss, androgenetic alopecia, is the most common and is typically genetic. A comprehensive history and exam with targeted laboratory testing can identify causes such as malnutrition, autoimmune and endocrine disease (Dakkak et al., 2024).

Shedding

Telogen effluvium is one of the most common causes of alopecia and is characterized by excessive shedding. Drugs, trauma and emotional or physiological stress can trigger it, and identifying and removing the cause is essential (Asghar et al., 2020).

Frequently Asked Questions

How can I tell if my part is normal or thinning?

Track it. A part that gradually widens over months, especially over the crown, can be a sign of female pattern hair loss.

Is a wide part always hair loss?

No. Part width varies. It's the change over time that matters.

When should I see a doctor?

If your part is widening, if shedding is heavy or continues, or if you notice patches of hair loss.
