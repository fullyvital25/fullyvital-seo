What Is Pygeum Bark?

Pygeum (Prunus africana) occurs in montane forests of sub-Saharan Africa and Madagascar. The stem bark is taken traditionally for conditions including benign prostatic hyperplasia (BPH).

What The Research Shows

A Combination Supplement Trial

Eighty patients aged 18 to 60 with chronic telogen effluvium or androgenetic alopecia were randomized to take one capsule daily of a supplement containing L-cystine, Serenoa repens, Cucurbita pepo, Pygeum africanum, vitamins and micronutrients, or placebo, for 6 months. Hair density rose by 9.9 hairs/cm² at 3 months and 12.3 at 6 months in the supplement group, significantly more than placebo, and overall hair volume was better. It was well tolerated, with no moderate or severe adverse events (Piquero-Casals et al., 2025).

What Pygeum Is

A review describes Prunus africana as occurring in montane forests of sub-Saharan Africa, traditionally used for BPH and other conditions, with anti-inflammatory, antimicrobial, antioxidant, anti-androgenic and other activities. The biological activity has largely been ascribed to phytosterols, mainly beta-sitosterol. The whole extract and single isolated compounds differed in activity (Rubegeta et al., 2023).

Use In Urology

A review of Pygeum africanum in urological practice says it is still used for lower urinary tract symptoms secondary to BPH, and rates the evidence level IV, based on expert opinion (Salinas-Casado et al., 2020).

Frequently Asked Questions

Does pygeum bark help hair loss?

A supplement containing pygeum plus other ingredients increased hair density in a placebo-controlled trial. Pygeum alone hasn't been tested for hair.

Does pygeum block DHT?

A review lists anti-androgenic activity among its reported activities, based on lab and animal work. We found no study showing it lowers DHT in people's scalps.

Is pygeum safe?

It was well tolerated in the trial. Talk to your doctor first.
