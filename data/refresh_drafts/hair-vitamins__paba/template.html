What Is PABA?

PABA is para-aminobenzoic acid. In medicine it has been given as potassium para-aminobenzoate (Potaba), used for sclerotic diseases, including Peyronie's disease of the penis.

Can PABA Darken Gray Hair?

Nobody can say. The 1950 report is old, its abstract isn't available, and later reviewers treat hair-colour effects of PABA as unconfirmed. Don't take PABA hoping to change your hair color.

What The Research Shows

The 1950 Report

A paper by Zarafonetis was titled "Darkening of gray hair during para-amino-benzoic acid therapy" (Zarafonetis, 1950). The PubMed record has no abstract, so we only report the title.

The 1992 Review

A review found that drug-induced hair colour changes are not a common adverse effect. A wide variety of drugs have been implicated, but very few have supporting data. Chloroquine and cancer chemotherapy drugs have the best evidence. Others, including p-aminobenzoic acid, await confirmatory data (Bublin & Thompson, 1992).

Safety Of The Oral Drug Form

A report compiling cases found that oral potassium para-aminobenzoate had been linked to acute liver injury in six individuals. The patient described recovered fully 4 months after stopping the drug, and the recommended management is to stop it immediately (Roy & Carrier, 2008).
