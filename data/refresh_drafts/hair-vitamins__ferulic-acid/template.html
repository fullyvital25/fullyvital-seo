Ferulic acid is a plant antioxidant found in vegetables, fruits, cereals and coffee. In a 2026 lab study, it boosted the growth of human hair-follicle cells and lengthened cultured human hair follicles.

Here's what that study found, how ferulic acid is used in skin care, and what it can and can't do for your scalp.

Key Takeaways

  • In cultured human dermal papilla cells and hair follicles, ferulic acid increased cell growth and mitochondrial activity, and lengthened follicles during the growth phase.
  • The effect looked estrogen-like: ferulic acid activated estrogen receptor alpha pathways in the cells.
  • In skin studies, ferulic acid stabilized a vitamin C and E solution and doubled its photoprotection.
  • We found no trial testing ferulic acid for hair growth in people.

What Is Ferulic Acid?

Ferulic acid is a common plant phenolic compound found in seeds and leaves and in plant cell walls. It's an abundant dietary antioxidant.

How Might Ferulic Acid Support Hair?

Hair Follicle Cells In The Lab

Researchers tested Cnidium officinale extract and its active compound, ferulic acid, on human dermal papilla cells (the cells that direct hair growth) and on cultured human hair follicles. Both significantly increased cell proliferation and mitochondrial activity, and extended follicle length during the anagen (growth) phase. Ferulic acid also raised c-JUN expression, a sign of estrogen-like receptor activation, and increased growth factors (Rim et al., 2026).

These are laboratory results. Clinical trials in people are still needed.

A Partner For Vitamins C And E On The Skin

In skin research, adding ferulic acid to a solution of 15% L-ascorbic acid and 1% alpha-tocopherol made the vitamins more stable and doubled protection against solar-simulated radiation, from about 4-fold to 8-fold (Lin et al., 2005). That was done on skin, not the scalp, and it tested a combination.

How To Get Ferulic Acid

High levels of ferulic acid are found in vegetables, fruits, cereals and coffee, and diet may supply an estimated 150 to 250 mg a day (Zhao & Moghadasian, 2008). We found no studies on ferulic acid supplements for hair, and no hair-specific topical dose.

Is Ferulic Acid Safe?

We didn't find safety assessments for concentrated supplements or scalp products. As with any topical product, patch test first and stop if your skin or scalp becomes irritated. Talk to your doctor if you're pregnant, breastfeeding, have a condition or take medication.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does ferulic acid help hair growth?

In the lab, ferulic acid increased the growth of human dermal papilla cells and lengthened cultured human hair follicles. It hasn't been tested for hair growth in people.

What foods contain ferulic acid?

Vegetables, fruits, cereals and coffee.

Can ferulic acid be used with vitamin C and E?

That's the combination best studied in skin, where ferulic acid improved the chemical stability of a vitamin C and E solution and doubled its photoprotection.
