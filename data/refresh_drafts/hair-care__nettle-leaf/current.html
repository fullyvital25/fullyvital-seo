What Is Stinging Nettle?

Stinging nettle is a perennial plant of temperate regions, used in traditional medicine for dermatological problems and hair loss, and studied for benign prostate hyperplasia.

Does Nettle Help Hair?

It's promising in the lab but unproven in people. The idea rests on its effect on 5α-reductase gene expression in skin cells, which isn't the same as growing hair.

What The Research Shows

A Review Of Natural Compounds

A 2023 review of medicinal plants and natural compounds for hair loss reported that extracts of Urtica dioica, Humulus lupulus, Serenoa repens, Vitis vinifera, Pygeum africanum and Cucurbita pepo, among others, may be effective in reducing hair loss directly or indirectly, thanks to anti-inflammatory and antioxidant properties. It said research suggests promise, not proof (Gasmi et al., 2023).

A Lab Study

Urtica dioica is reported to have therapeutic activity against benign prostate hyperplasia, mainly through 5α-reductase inhibition. In cultured HaCaT skin cells treated with leaf extract, expression of the 5α-reductase type II gene fell significantly (p = 0.0021) to 0.587 fold. The authors suggested potential in cosmetic dermatology against androgenic skin diseases; this was a cell study (Pekmezci & Türkoğlu, 2023).

Contact Reactions

Skin contact with the common stinging nettle is a frequent cause of contact urticaria, with a stinging sensation lasting more than 12 hours. Nettle hair contains histamine and serotonin (Oliver et al., 1991).

Frequently Asked Questions

Does stinging nettle help hair growth?

We found no clinical trial. Lab work shows nettle leaf extract lowered a 5α-reductase gene's expression in skin cells.

Is stinging nettle safe on the skin?

Fresh nettle stings and causes contact urticaria. Patch test any nettle product.

Does nettle block DHT?

Not shown in people. The lab study measured gene expression for 5α-reductase in cells.
