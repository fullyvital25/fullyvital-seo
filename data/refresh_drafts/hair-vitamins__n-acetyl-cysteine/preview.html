N-Acetyl Cysteine (NAC) for Hair: Glutathione, Oxidative Stress & What to Know

N-acetyl cysteine, better known as NAC, has become one of the most talked-about supplements for healthy aging, and hair is part of that conversation. Its main claim to fame is simple and well established: NAC helps your body make glutathione, its master antioxidant.

Here's how NAC works, what the research says about oxidative stress and hair, and what to know before you try it.

Key Takeaways

  • NAC is a well-studied compound with a long safety record. It's used in hospitals and widely sold as a supplement.
  • It works mainly by raising levels of glutathione, the body's most important internal antioxidant, and it also has anti-inflammatory effects.
  • People who gray early tend to have lower glutathione and higher oxidative stress markers.
  • In a 16-week clinical trial in older adults, NAC combined with glycine corrected glutathione deficiency and lowered oxidative stress.

What Is N-Acetyl Cysteine?

NAC is a modified form of the amino acid cysteine. Doctors have used it for decades, as an antidote for acetaminophen (paracetamol) overdose and to thin mucus.

How NAC Supports Your Hair

NAC's main antioxidant role comes from its ability to raise glutathione levels inside cells. By helping replenish glutathione, NAC supports the body's own antioxidant defenses, which is why it features in many hair and healthy-aging formulas. No large trial has yet measured NAC's effect on hair growth or color directly.

What The Research Shows

It Helps Your Body Make Glutathione

NAC can raise glutathione levels inside cells. Glutathione is the key molecule that keeps the cell's redox balance in check. NAC also reduces inflammatory signals such as TNF-α, IL-6 and IL-1β, and it has a well-established safety profile (Tenório et al., 2021).

Oxidative Stress And Early Graying

Hair follicles generate oxidative stress as a by-product of making pigment. In a study of people who go gray early (before age 25), blood levels of oxidative damage markers were higher and glutathione was lower than in people without early graying. The more gray hair, the bigger the imbalance (Saxena et al., 2020).

What Clinical Research Shows

In a randomized, placebo-controlled trial, older adults took GlyNAC (glycine plus NAC) or a placebo for 16 weeks. GlyNAC corrected their glutathione deficiency and improved oxidative stress, inflammation and several markers of aging. It was safe and well tolerated (Kumar et al., 2023). That trial didn't look at hair.

Is NAC Safe?

NAC is generally safe, with few side effects. As with any supplement, check with your doctor first if you're pregnant, breastfeeding, have a medical condition or take prescription medication.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is NAC good for hair?

NAC helps your body make glutathione and reduces inflammatory signals. Oxidative stress is linked to early graying, which is why NAC is used in hair-support formulas. Direct hair growth trials are still to come.

Does NAC help with gray hair?

People who gray early tend to have lower glutathione and more oxidative stress. NAC raises glutathione, but no study has yet tested whether it changes hair color.

How long does NAC take to work?

In the GlyNAC trial, improvements in glutathione were measured over 16 weeks.
