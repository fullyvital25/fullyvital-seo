Soy Isoflavones for Hair: What the Research Shows

Soy isoflavones are plant compounds called phytoestrogens, and they turn up in hair supplements. The honest hair evidence is thin: a systematic review of soy in dermatology lists hair parameters among many outcomes, a small pilot trial used a multi-ingredient supplement that included isoflavones, and an animal study found no clear effect on hair loss.

Key Takeaways

  • A 2023 systematic review of 30 studies of soy supplements and soy skin products reported benefits across skin parameters, including hair and nail parameters.
  • In a 24-week pilot of 10 people with androgenetic alopecia, a multi-ingredient supplement containing soy isoflavones increased terminal hair count and hair mass index. It can't show what isoflavones did alone.
  • In mice, diets higher in soy oil lowered the rate of alopecia areata onset, but the genistein injection test didn't show a significant link to hair loss extent.
  • We found no trial of soy isoflavones alone for hair in people.

What Are Soy Isoflavones?

Isoflavones are phytoestrogens in soybeans, including genistein. They are used in foods and supplements, including some for hair.

Do Soy Isoflavones Help Hair?

It's not established. The three sources below each say something different, and none isolates isoflavones in a hair trial.

What The Research Shows

A Systematic Review Of Soy In Dermatology

A review of 30 studies (13 oral, 17 topical) found soy supplementation or application showed efficacy for many dermatologic parameters, including hair and nail parameters, skin barrier, hydration and photoaging. Wrinkle and aging parameters were the most frequently assessed outcomes, and the abstract gives no detail on hair (Natarelli et al., 2023).

A Multi-Ingredient Pilot

Ten adults with androgenetic alopecia took a supplement (Forti5) containing green tea extract, omega-3 and omega-6 fatty acids, vitamin D3, melatonin, beta-sitosterol and soy isoflavones for 24 weeks. Eight of ten were rated improved, with terminal hair count up 5.9% and hair mass index up 9.5%. It was open-label with no control group (Nichols et al., 2017).

A Mouse Study

In mice grafted with alopecia areata skin, 86%, 39% and 18% developed alopecia areata on 1%, 5% and 20% soy oil diets. Mice injected with genistein had fewer cases (4 of 10 vs 9 of 10), but there was no significant association between the extent of hair loss and diet or genistein (McElwee et al., 2003).

Frequently Asked Questions

Do soy isoflavones help hair growth?

We found no study of isoflavones alone for hair in people. A pilot of a multi-ingredient supplement that included them showed improvement, with no control group.

Do soy isoflavones cause hair loss?

We found no study showing that they do.

Are soy isoflavones safe?

Talk to your doctor before taking them, especially if you are pregnant, breastfeeding or have a hormone-sensitive condition.
