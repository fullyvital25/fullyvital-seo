Here's what the research says about heat and hair, why wet hair is more vulnerable, and how to limit damage.

Key Takeaways

  • Hair crimping uses a heated iron to press waves into the hair. Studies of heat styling show damage to hair, not benefit.
  • In a lab study of a flat iron on virgin hair, heat damaged the cuticle and caused protein loss, and keratin began to denature at 237 °C.
  • Heat treatments cause chemical damage in dry hair, and in wet hair they cause the same chemical damage plus considerably more structural damage.
  • Heat and UV exposure disrupted disulphide bonds in hair keratins, which are crucial for structural integrity.

What Is Hair Crimping?

Hair crimping creates small, regular waves in the hair with a heated crimping iron. It's a styling technique, and the result lasts until the next wash.

Does Crimping Help Hair Growth?

No evidence says so. Styling heat acts on the hair shaft, and we found no study showing it affects how hair grows from the follicle. The idea that crimping promotes growth isn't supported.

What The Research Shows

Heat And The Hair Cuticle

In a lab study, a flat iron was used on Caucasian and Asian virgin hair. Heat damaged the cuticle, changed the protein structure and caused protein loss, which was greater in Asian hair than in Caucasian hair. The keratin's denaturation temperature was 237 °C. The authors stressed the importance of temperature control (Lima et al., 2019).

Wet Versus Dry Hair

Researchers compared heat protection sprays on hair treated with straightening irons. Repeated applications of both a water-based and an ethanol-based heat-protection spray reduced tryptophan (chemical) damage, but the water-based "wet" spray led to greater structural damage and weaker hair in tensile tests. The authors concluded that dry hair suffers chemical damage and some structural damage from heat, while wet hair suffers the same chemical damage with considerably more structural damage (Christian et al., 2011).

Heat, UV And Keratin

Human hair exposed to accelerated heat or UV treatments, simulating about a year of styling tools and sun, developed disulphide-bond-disrupting changes (cysteic acid and dehydroalanine) in two major hair keratins. The authors noted these changes are crucial for keratin structure and may contribute to the loss of structural integrity seen after repeated styling (Plowman et al., 2025).

Frequently Asked Questions

Does crimping damage hair?

Heat styling damages the cuticle and keratin in lab studies. Crimping irons weren't specifically tested in these studies, which used flat irons.

Does crimping make hair grow?

We found no evidence that it does.

How can I crimp with less damage?

Style on dry hair, keep heat low, and use a heat-protection product.
