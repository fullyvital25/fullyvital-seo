What Is Saw Palmetto?

Saw palmetto (Serenoa repens) is a palm whose berry extract is sold as a supplement, often for hair and prostate health. In hair research, doses of 100 to 320 mg have been studied in oral and topical forms.

Does Saw Palmetto Help Hair?

Possibly, for some people with pattern hair thinning. The evidence is limited and many studies were small, so it is not a substitute for a doctor's diagnosis or for approved treatments.

Using Saw Palmetto Sensibly

  • See a doctor first if you are losing hair. Pattern thinning and other causes need different approaches.
  • Be patient and track it. Trials ran for months to two years.
  • Tell your doctor you take it, especially if you use other hair-loss medicines or hormonal products.

What The Research Shows

Systematic Review

A systematic review searched for studies of saw palmetto in alopecia and found five randomized trials and two prospective cohort studies of topical and oral products containing 100 to 320 mg. Reported results included 60% improvement in overall hair quality, a 27% improvement in total hair count, increased hair density in 83.3% of patients, and stabilized progression in 52%. Saw palmetto was well tolerated, with no serious adverse events. The authors cautioned that robust high-quality data are lacking, and that many products combined saw palmetto with other ingredients (Evron et al., 2020).

Compared With Finasteride

In an open-label study, 100 men with mild to moderate androgenetic alopecia took either 320 mg of Serenoa repens daily or 1 mg of finasteride daily for 24 months. Based on global photos, 38% of the saw palmetto group had increased hair growth, against 68% of the finasteride group. The authors observed that finasteride acted at both the front and vertex, while saw palmetto acted mainly at the vertex (Rossi et al., 2012). Finasteride is a prescription medicine; talk to a doctor about it.

Placebo-Controlled Trial

In a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial, 60 adults with self-perceived thinning hair (half women) took a proprietary saw palmetto fatty acid extract (n = 40) or placebo (n = 20). At 90 days, terminal and total hair counts were significantly higher than placebo in front and back areas, and no adverse events were reported. These are preliminary results of a 6-month study, and the extract was a specific proprietary product (Ablon, 2025).

Frequently Asked Questions

Does saw palmetto block DHT?

It is described as having antiandrogenic properties, but the hair trials measured hair outcomes, not hormones.

How long does saw palmetto take to work?

Studies ran from 90 days to 24 months, so expect it to take months, if it helps at all.

Is saw palmetto safe?

The systematic review found it well tolerated, but long-term adverse events were not established. Check with your doctor.
