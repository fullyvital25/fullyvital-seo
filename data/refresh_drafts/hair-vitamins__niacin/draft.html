Key Takeaways

  • In a 6-month, placebo-controlled pilot of 60 women with female pattern hair loss, topical niacin derivatives (octyl and tetradecyl nicotinate) significantly increased hair fullness compared with placebo.
  • A 2020 paper found niacinamide could not stimulate VEGF synthesis in a lab assay and concluded it does not stimulate hair growth based on existing evidence.
  • Oral niacin and nicotinamide are linked to side effects including stomach upset, liver dysfunction, flushing and rash, and these are dose-dependent.
  • We found no trial of oral niacin for hair growth.

What Is Niacin?

Niacin is a form of vitamin B3, along with nicotinamide (niacinamide). Different forms behave differently, so a result for one doesn't apply to another.

Does Niacin Help Hair?

It depends on the form. A pilot of topical niacin derivatives was positive; topical niacinamide was not supported; and oral niacin has not been tested for hair.

What The Research Shows

Topical Niacin Derivatives

Sixty women with Ludwig type I to III female pattern hair loss were randomized to a topical niacin derivative (40 active, 20 placebo) for 6 months. Blinded photographic analysis showed a statistically significant increase in hair fullness versus placebo (P = 0.04). The authors called it a pilot and said it warrants further study (Draelos et al., 2005).

Topical Niacinamide

A letter tested whether niacinamide stimulates VEGF synthesis in vitro, found it did not across a broad range of doses, and concluded that topical niacinamide does not stimulate hair growth based on existing evidence (Oblong et al., 2020).

Oral Niacin Side Effects

An overview of 14 reviews found oral niacin and nicotinamide were associated with adverse effects including stomach upset, liver dysfunction, flushing, skin rash and fatigue. Nicotinamide had a lower incidence of side effects than niacin, effects were more common at higher doses, and most diminished or resolved when the dose was reduced or stopped (Young & Gazzard, 2025).

Frequently Asked Questions

Does niacin help hair growth?

A pilot found topical niacin derivatives increased hair fullness in women, but topical niacinamide wasn't supported and oral niacin hasn't been tested for hair.

Is niacin safe?

Oral niacin can cause flushing, stomach upset and liver effects, and these are dose-dependent. Talk to your doctor first.

Is niacinamide the same as niacin?

No. They are different forms of vitamin B3 and have different effects and side-effect profiles.
