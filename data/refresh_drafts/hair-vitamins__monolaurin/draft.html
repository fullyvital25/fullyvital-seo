What Is Monolaurin?

Monolaurin is a compound derived from lauric acid, a fatty acid in coconut oil. It's sold as a dietary supplement and studied for its effects on microbes.

Does Monolaurin Help Your Scalp Or Hair?

No study we found tested it on the scalp. It's reasonable to be curious because a healthy scalp matters for hair, but the monolaurin studies are of bacteria from other skin conditions, not dandruff or hair loss.

What The Research Shows

Lab Studies On Skin Bacteria

In samples from children and teens with infected skin conditions, 20 mg/ml of monolaurin showed 100% sensitivity in Staphylococcus aureus, Streptococcus and coagulase-negative Staphylococcus. These bacteria showed no resistance to it. It was a lab study, and the authors said further studies were needed before clinical trials (Carpo et al., 2007).

Resistant Bacteria In Atopic Dermatitis

In a 2025 study, S. aureus swabbed from 60 people with atopic dermatitis included antibiotic-resistant isolates. Monolaurin inhibited them at a concentration of 2 µg/mL without cytotoxicity to cultured epidermal and dermal cells (Laowansiri et al., 2025).

What A Review Of The Evidence Found

A review of the clinical use of monolaurin found many articles on its antimicrobial effects in vitro, but only three peer-reviewed papers showing antimicrobial effects in humans, and those were for intravaginal and intraoral use. It found no peer-reviewed evidence for clinical use of monolaurin as a human dietary supplement other than as a nutrient (Barker et al., 2019).

Frequently Asked Questions

Does monolaurin help hair or scalp health?

We found no study of monolaurin on hair or the scalp. The research is on skin bacteria in the lab.

Is monolaurin safe?

In the 2025 study it wasn't toxic to cultured skin cells. For use in people as a supplement, a review found no peer-reviewed clinical evidence beyond its role as a nutrient. Talk to your doctor first.

There's no evidence it can. It has not been tested on the scalp.
