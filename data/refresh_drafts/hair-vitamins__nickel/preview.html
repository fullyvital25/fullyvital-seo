  • Jewellery and metal in clothing are still the main causes of nickel allergy and dermatitis.

What Is Nickel?

Nickel is a metal found in jewellery, metal items, food and the environment. For most people, it matters for skin allergy rather than for hair.

Does Nickel Affect Hair?

We found no study of nickel as a treatment for hair growth, and none showing normal exposure causes hair loss. If you suspect a nickel allergy, see a dermatologist.

What The Research Shows

Nickel Allergy

A clinical review describes nickel as the most frequent cause of contact allergy worldwide. A European nickel regulation reduced prevalence, but allergy still affects approximately 8% to 19% of adults and 8% to 10% of children and adolescents, with a strong female predominance. Jewellery and metal in clothing remain the main causes (Ahlström et al., 2019).

Nickel In Food

Nickel intake from the Danish diet was estimated at 150 micrograms per person per day. Roots, vegetables, grain and bread supply much of it, while cocoa and chocolate, soya beans, oatmeal, nuts, almonds and legumes have very high nickel content. Large amounts of these foods can raise intake to 900 micrograms or more a day. Whether dietary nickel can flare hand eczema in nickel-allergic people was an open question that needs provocation studies (Flyvholm et al., 1984).

Nickel In Hair

In 265 young adults (87 males and 178 females), researchers analyzed hair minerals and linked them with questionnaires. They concluded that nickel exposure can come from lifestyle, eating habits and environmental sources (Michalak et al., 2012).

Frequently Asked Questions

Does nickel cause hair loss?

We found no study showing that it does.

Is nickel good for hair?

We found no study showing a benefit. Nickel's main health relevance in the research is allergy.

What foods are high in nickel?

Cocoa and chocolate, soya beans, oatmeal, nuts and almonds, and fresh and dried legumes have very high nickel contents.
