What Is Uva Ursi?

Uva ursi (Arctostaphylos uva-ursi, bearberry) is a plant whose leaf is used in herbal products. Its best-known compound is arbutin.

Does Uva Ursi Do Anything For Skin Or Hair?

For skin, the evidence is lab-based. For hair, there is none. If you see uva ursi in a scalp product, know that its benefits for hair haven't been tested.

What The Research Shows

Acne Bacteria In The Lab

Uva ursi leaf extract was tested against Cutibacterium acnes in dishes. It inhibited the bacteria (MIC90 0.6 mg/mL), and the researchers also studied its effects on skin cells and inflammatory signals. Its permeation through skin was followed by tracking arbutin (Dell'Annunziata et al., 2022).

Melanin Enzyme

Extracts of six Arctostaphylos species were tested for effects on melanin synthesis. Two species, A. patula and A. viscida, strongly inhibited tyrosinase, and the authors suggested they could be used as skin-whitening agents (Matsuda et al., 1996). Whether uva ursi affects hair pigment hasn't been studied.

Safety Of Arbutin And Hydroquinone

Arbutin from uva ursi is absorbed and converted to hydroquinone conjugates. A review estimated that a recommended daily dose liberates free hydroquinone at up to 11 µg/kg body weight per day, and noted that dietary sources of arbutin and hydroquinone regularly consumed long term create comparable exposure levels. It found no direct human evidence that free hydroquinone causes convulsions, liver or kidney damage or tumor promotion (de Arriba et al., 2013).

Frequently Asked Questions

Does uva ursi help hair?

What does uva ursi do for skin?

Is uva ursi safe?

A review of the data on its hydroquinone found no direct human evidence that free hydroquinone causes convulsions, liver or kidney damage. Talk to your doctor before taking it, especially if you're pregnant, breastfeeding, have a condition or take medication.
