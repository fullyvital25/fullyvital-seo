What Is Vitamin B12?

Vitamin B12 (cobalamin) is a water-soluble vitamin that is important for blood and the nervous system.

Does B12 Help Hair?

Only, as far as we found, where there is a deficiency. For others, we found no evidence that extra B12 helps hair.

Using B12 Sensibly

  • Ask your doctor to test if you have risk factors or symptoms of deficiency.
  • Don't self-treat hair loss with B12. Many causes exist.
  • Tell your doctor about injections or high doses. The review notes rare skin reactions to B12 therapy.

What The Research Shows

B12 And The Skin

A review of vitamin B12 in dermatology states that altered cobalamin levels can cause skin changes. The cutaneous signs of deficiency include hyperpigmentation (most common), hair and nail changes and oral changes such as glossitis. Complications of cobalamin therapy include acne, rosacea and allergic site reactions, and anaphylaxis with injections (Brescoll and Daveluy, 2015).

Women With Diffuse Hair Loss

In 108 women with diffuse hair loss, split into acute and chronic telogen effluvium, vitamin B12 levels were similar between the groups and had no significant correlation with phototrichogram findings. Ferritin was found to have an important role in diffuse hair loss (Bilik et al., 2024). This study didn't include a no-hair-loss comparison group.

Deficiency Signs

A review of micronutrient deficiencies noted they commonly cause abnormalities of skin, hair and nails, and may be present in people with impaired absorption or poor dietary intake. B vitamins including riboflavin, niacin, pyridoxine, biotin and B12 are among those most commonly presenting with skin findings (DiBaise and Tarleton, 2019).

Frequently Asked Questions

Does low B12 cause hair loss?

Reviews list hair changes among signs of deficiency, but a study in women with telogen effluvium found no link between B12 level and hair findings.

Should I take B12 for hair growth?

We found no trial showing it helps hair if you aren't deficient. Ask your doctor.

Who is at risk of low B12?

The review mentions impaired absorption or poor dietary intake.
