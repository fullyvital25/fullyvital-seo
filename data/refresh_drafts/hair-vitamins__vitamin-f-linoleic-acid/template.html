What Is Linoleic Acid?

Linoleic acid (18:2, omega-6) is an essential fatty acid, meaning the body needs it from the diet. It's incorporated into skin lipids, including ceramides that support the skin barrier.

Possibly, based on lab work, but it hasn't been shown in people. The idea comes from cell studies, not trials.

What The Research Shows

A Review Of Linoleic Acid In Skin And Hair

A 2024 review describes linoleic acid as an essential fatty acid that can be metabolized into other omega-6 fatty acids and incorporated into complex lipids, including omega-hydroxy-ceramides. It reports that animal and skin-cell studies suggest linoleic acid or linoleic-acid-rich oils, applied topically, have effects including skin barrier repair, photoprotection, anti-inflammatory effects and stimulation of hair growth. It calls for more investigation of clinical application (Wang et al., 2024).

Hair Follicle Cells In The Lab

In human follicle dermal papilla cells, linoleic acid activated Wnt/β-catenin signalling, increased cyclin D1 and several growth factors (VEGF, IGF-1, HGF, KGF) in a dose-dependent way, and significantly inhibited DKK-1, a primary signalling molecule in dihydrotestosterone-related alopecia (Ryu et al., 2021). This was cell research.

Which Oils

A review of natural oils for skin-barrier repair found that oils with a higher linoleic-to-oleic acid ratio have better barrier-repair potential, whereas oils with more oleic acid, which is irritating, may be detrimental. Cold pressing preserves beneficial lipids (Vaughn et al., 2018). That review was about skin, not hair.
