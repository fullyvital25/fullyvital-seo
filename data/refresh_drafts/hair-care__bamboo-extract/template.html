Bamboo extract is often sold as a source of silica for hair. We found no study of bamboo extract on human hair. The closest research is on silicon supplements, and those trials used a specific form called choline-stabilized orthosilicic acid, not bamboo.

Key Takeaways

  • We found no human hair study of bamboo extract itself.

What Is Bamboo Extract?

Bamboo extract is a plant extract used in hair and supplement products, usually described as a source of silica. Silicon is a trace element found in the body, and supplement makers use it to explain why bamboo is added.

Using Silicon Products Sensibly

  • Check the form. Absorption varies a lot between forms of silicon, so a bamboo label tells you little about how much your body gets.
  • Look at the whole picture. Hair that breaks or looks thin can have causes a doctor can check, such as nutrition or a health condition.
  • Talk to your doctor before adding supplements, especially if you take medicines or have a health condition.

What The Research Shows

Hair Thickness And Strength

In a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled study, 48 women with fine hair took 10 mg of silicon a day as choline-stabilized orthosilicic acid, or placebo, for 9 months. Hair cross-sectional area increased significantly from baseline in the silicon group but not in the placebo group. Break load changed significantly in the placebo group (-10.8%) but not in the silicon group (-2.20%), and elasticity fell less with silicon (-4.52% against -11.9%). The authors concluded the supplement had a positive effect on tensile strength and resulted in thicker hair (Wickett et al., 2007).

Brittleness

A second randomized trial gave 50 women with sun-damaged facial skin 10 mg of silicon a day, in the same form, or placebo for 20 weeks. The main outcomes were skin measures. Volunteers also scored hair and nail brittleness on a scale, and scores were significantly lower after 20 weeks in the silicon group compared with their own baseline (Barel et al., 2005). These were self-ratings, and hair was a secondary outcome.

Which Form Is Absorbed

A review of silicon for skin and hair care found that silicon supplements vary widely in bioavailability, from under 1% to close to 50%, depending on the chemical form. Orthosilicic acid had the highest bioavailability. The authors noted that clinical studies evaluating safety and efficacy are still lacking (Araújo et al., 2016).

Frequently Asked Questions

Does bamboo extract help hair grow?

We found no study showing that. The silicon research used another form, and it measured hair thickness and strength, not growth.

Is silica good for hair?

One placebo-controlled trial in women with fine hair found thicker hair fibres and smaller strength losses with a silicon supplement. More studies are needed.

Are silicon supplements safe?

A review said clinical studies of safety are still lacking. Talk to your doctor first.
