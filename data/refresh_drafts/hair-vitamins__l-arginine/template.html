Arginine is an amino acid with a new and interesting hair finding: a 2025 study found that people with androgenetic alopecia had markedly low serum arginine, and arginine supplementation restored hair growth in a mouse model and in cultured human hair follicles. That is lab and animal work. We found no trial of oral arginine for hair in people.

Here's what that study found, what else is known about arginine and the follicle, and how much has been tested for safety.

Key Takeaways

  • In a 2025 study, arginine had the most pronounced reduction among all amino acids in the serum of people with androgenetic alopecia compared with healthy controls.

What Is Arginine?

Arginine is an amino acid. In the body it is converted by nitric oxide synthases to citrulline and nitric oxide.

Does Arginine Help Hair?

Possibly, but it's early. The 2025 study identified arginine shortage as a driver in pattern hair loss and suggests replenishing it locally. That's very different from taking an arginine pill and expecting hair.

Using Arginine

  • Be realistic. No trial shows oral arginine grows hair.

What The Research Shows

Arginine And Androgenetic Alopecia

Using serum metabolomics, researchers found a strikingly different metabolic signature in people with androgenetic alopecia, with arginine showing the most pronounced reduction among amino acids. In balding follicles, the arginine transporter SLC7A1 was lower and the arginine-breaking enzyme ARG2 higher. Adding arginine, or blocking ARG2, rescued hair growth in a mouse model and in cultured human hair follicles, and a microneedle-based arginine delivery system was effective in humanized models. The authors called localized arginine replacement a promising clinical strategy (Duan et al., 2025).

Nitric Oxide And The Follicle

Nitric oxide is made from arginine by nitric oxide synthases. In samples of haired scalp from 10 healthy people, different NOS forms were found in different melanocyte populations and in the inner root sheath of the hair follicle, suggesting nitric oxide may influence several aspects of hair follicle biology (Sowden et al., 2005).

How Much Has Been Tested

In a randomized, placebo-controlled trial, 101 overweight or obese but otherwise healthy adults took 0, 15 or 30 g of arginine a day for 90 days. Serum arginine rose with dose, and people tolerated 15 and 30 g a day without adverse events (McNeal et al., 2018). The trial did not look at hair.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does L-arginine help hair growth?

In the lab, arginine rescued hair growth in a mouse model and cultured human follicles. No trial shows oral arginine does so in people.

Is arginine safe?

A 90-day trial found 15 and 30 g a day tolerated in adults without adverse events. Talk to your doctor before taking it.

Can low arginine cause hair loss?

In one study, people with androgenetic alopecia had low serum arginine. That's an association, and it does not show that low arginine causes hair loss.
