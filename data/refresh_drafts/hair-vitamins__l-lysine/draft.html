What The Research Shows

Lysine And Iron In Shedding

A review of nutritional factors and hair loss concluded that the role of the essential amino acid L-lysine in hair loss also appears to be important. Double-blind data confirmed the findings of an open study in women with increased hair shedding, where a significant proportion responded to L-lysine and iron therapy. The same review found no evidence that low serum zinc causes hair loss and warned that excessive supplement intake may itself cause hair loss (Rushton, 2002).

Chronic Shedding In Women

A second review from the same group reported that in women the major cause of hair loss before age 50 is nutritional, affecting 30%. Increased persistent shedding (chronic telogen effluvium) and reduced hair volume were the principal changes, and the main cause appeared to be depleted iron stores compromised by a suboptimal intake of L-lysine. The authors noted it can take many months to redress (Rushton et al., 2002). Both reviews come from the same research group.

How Much Has Been Tested

A systematic review of 71 articles with 3,357 subjects looked at oral L-lysine supplementation, with doses up to 17.5 g a day and dosing periods from 1 to 1,095 days. Adverse events were mainly subjective gastrointestinal symptoms, and the risk of GI symptoms was not significantly higher (risk ratio 1.02). The provisional no-observed-adverse-effect level was 6.0 g a day (Hayamizu et al., 2020). That review wasn't about hair.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does L-lysine help hair growth?

Reviews report that women with increased shedding responded to L-lysine plus iron. Lysine on its own hasn't been shown to grow hair.

Is L-lysine safe?

A systematic review found mild GI symptoms mainly and set a provisional safe level at 6.0 g a day. Talk to your doctor first.

Should I take lysine and iron together?

That is a question for your doctor. Excess supplements can cause problems, and iron needs depend on your levels.
