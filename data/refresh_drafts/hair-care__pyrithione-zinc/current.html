What The Research Shows

Hair Count Trial

In a 6-month randomized, investigator-blinded study of 200 men aged 18 to 49 with Hamilton-Norwood type III vertex or type IV baldness, a daily 1% pyrithione zinc shampoo, 5% minoxidil solution, placebo shampoo, or both active treatments were compared. Total hair counts rose significantly with pyrithione zinc, minoxidil and the combination versus placebo after 9 weeks. The increase with pyrithione zinc was slightly less than half that of minoxidil and was maintained over 26 weeks. There was no advantage to using both. The authors concluded it gave a modest, sustained improvement in hair growth (Berger et al., 2003).

Dandruff Research

A meta-analysis of 17 half-head, double-blind, randomized studies (2,088 people) compared 1% ZnPT shampoo with a non-anti-dandruff shampoo over 4 weeks. The response to ZnPT was greater in men than in women, with a 4-week between-treatment difference in flake score of -17.5 in men and -11.1 in women. The authors linked this to sex-related differences in the scalp's stratum corneum (Collins et al., 2018).

Allergy

A 1985 case report was titled "Zinc pyrithione (Zinc Omadine) allergy" (Brandrup & Menné, 1985). The PubMed record has no abstract, so we only report the title.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does pyrithione zinc help hair loss?

In a 200-man trial, a 1% pyrithione zinc shampoo modestly increased hair count versus placebo. It was less effective than 5% minoxidil.

Does pyrithione zinc work for dandruff?

A meta-analysis of 17 studies found 1% ZnPT shampoo reduced dandruff more than a non-anti-dandruff shampoo.

Can you be allergic to pyrithione zinc?

Allergy has been reported. Stop use if you notice irritation and see a doctor.
