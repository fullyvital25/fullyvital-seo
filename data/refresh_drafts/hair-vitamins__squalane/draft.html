What Is Squalane?

Squalane is a saturated derivative of squalene. Squalene is found in shark liver oil and, in smaller amounts, some vegetable oils, and it's a constituent of human sebum.

Does Squalane Help Hair?

Squalane is best understood as a conditioning and carrier ingredient. It can make a product feel better and may help other ingredients stay on the skin, but it isn't a hair-growth active.

What The Research Shows

What Squalane Is And Does

Squalene occurs in shark liver oil and some vegetable oils, and human sebum contains 13% squalene as a major constituent. Squalane is its saturated derivative. Reported activities in animal models and the lab include antioxidant, drug-carrier, skin-hydrating and emollient effects (Kim & Karadeniz, 2012).

Squalane On The Hair Surface

In a study of human hair using atomic force microscopy, squalane was used as the test liquid to probe wetting. Removing fatty acids from the cuticle surface changed hair wettability at the nanometre scale (Dupres et al., 2007). This was a surface physics study using squalane as a probe, not a test of squalane as a treatment.

Squalane As A Carrier In Skin

In ex vivo skin, adding squalane to a water-in-oil emulsion of phenolic compounds decreased their permeability coefficients, meaning squalane increased the retention of the polyphenols in the skin. The authors suggested it as an efficient vehicle for increasing dermal availability (Oliveira et al., 2022).

Frequently Asked Questions

Does squalane help hair growth?

We found no study showing that it does.

What does squalane do for hair?

It's used as an emollient. Studies of its hair effects are not available.

Is squalane safe?

Patch test any new product and stop if you notice irritation. Talk to your doctor if you have a skin condition.
