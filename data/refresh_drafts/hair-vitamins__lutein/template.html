What Is Lutein?

Lutein is a xanthophyll, a type of carotenoid. It's found in plants and, in the body, in tissues like the eye. Its relevance to hair is indirect: it's an antioxidant, and antioxidants come up in hair conversations. That link hasn't been tested for lutein.

What The Research Shows

What Lutein Does

In humans, lutein is believed to work in two main ways: as a filter of high-energy blue light and as an antioxidant that scavenges reactive oxygen species. It's deposited in the lens and macula of the eye, and it may also protect skin from UV-induced damage (Alves-Rodrigues & Shao, 2004).

Skin Tone Trial

Fifty healthy adults with mild-to-moderate dry skin were randomized to a daily supplement of 10 mg lutein and 2 mg zeaxanthin isomers or a placebo for 12 weeks (46 finished). Overall skin tone and skin lightness improved significantly in the lutein group versus placebo, and their minimal erythemal dose, a sunburn threshold, increased (Juturu et al., 2016). The study looked at skin, not hair.

Food Sources

A review described diet models that reach 5 mg and 10 mg of lutein and zeaxanthin a day through food alone. The important points: nutrient-rich sources, cooking methods, dietary variety and healthy fats. The foods include dark green leafy vegetables, pistachio nuts and eggs (Eisenhauer et al., 2017).
