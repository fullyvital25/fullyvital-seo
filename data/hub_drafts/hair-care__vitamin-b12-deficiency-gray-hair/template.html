Low vitamin B12 is one of the nutrient shortfalls most consistently found in people who go gray early. It doesn't cause most graying, which is genetic, but if you're graying young it's worth checking your B12, because a real deficiency is common, easy to test and worth correcting for your overall health.

This guide explains how B12 is linked to gray hair, the other signs of a deficiency, whether supplements help, and what to ask your doctor.

Key fact: Only a blood test can tell you whether your B12 is low. Gray hair on its own isn't a reliable sign.

The Short Answer

  • People who gray early have been found to have lower vitamin B12 levels than people who don't.
  • Low folate and biotin, and an underactive thyroid, have also been linked to early graying.
  • B12 deficiency has other signs, including skin darkening, hair and nail changes, and a sore, smooth tongue.
  • Correct a deficiency that a blood test confirms. No study shows extra B12 changes graying in people whose levels are already normal.

What Does Vitamin B12 Do?

Vitamin B12 (cobalamin) is a water-soluble vitamin your body needs to make blood cells and keep the nervous system working. It also affects the skin and hair, which is why low levels can show up there.

Is Vitamin B12 Deficiency Linked to Gray Hair?

Yes, in people who gray early. Several studies of young people with premature graying have found lower B12 levels than in people without it. That's an association, not proof that low B12 caused the graying, and most graying is still down to genetics.

B12 rarely acts alone. The same studies point to low folate and biotin, and to thyroid problems, which is why a check-up usually looks at more than one thing. Which vitamins help with gray hair and gray hair and nutrient depletion by age.

Other Signs of Low B12

  • Darkening of the skin (the most common skin sign)
  • Changes in the hair and nails
  • A sore, red or smooth tongue, and mouth ulcers

If you have several of these, ask your doctor for a B12 test rather than guessing.

Who Is More Likely to Have Low B12?

B12 comes mainly from animal foods, so if you eat little or none of them it's worth paying closer attention. Your doctor can tell you whether you're at higher risk of running low.

What the Research Shows

A case-control study of 52 people under 20 with premature graying (Daulatabad et al., 2017) found clearly lower vitamin B12 than in controls: an average of 198 pg/ml against 343 pg/ml. Folic acid and biotin levels were also significantly lower. The authors call for larger studies.

A review of 71 people whose graying started before 25 (Sonthalia et al., 2017) found that low vitamin B12 and an underactive thyroid were both significantly associated with early graying, while anemia, iron stores and blood sugar were not. Graying started at an average age of about 10, and 90% had a parent or sibling who also grayed early.

A 2015 review of vitamin B12 in dermatology (Brescoll and Daveluy) describes the skin signs of deficiency: most commonly skin darkening, along with hair and nail changes and changes in the mouth. It also notes that B12 treatment can occasionally cause acne, rosacea or allergic reactions, mainly with injections.

Should You Take B12 for Gray Hair?

Only if you're low. The steps that make sense:

  1. Get tested, especially if you're graying in your teens or 20s or have other signs of deficiency.
  2. If you're deficient, follow your doctor's advice on dose and form, and look for the cause.
  3. Give it time. Any effect on hair color would only show in new growth, over months.
  4. Don't megadose. No study shows that extra B12 changes graying when your levels are already normal.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long does B12 take to reverse gray hair?

There's no reliable timeline, because no study has shown that B12 reverses gray hair. If a deficiency is corrected, any change would appear only in new growth, so judge it over several months.

Why is my gray hair turning dark again?

Some gray hairs can regain color naturally, for example after a stressful period passes or an underlying cause is treated. It's uncommon and usually partial.

What vitamin am I lacking if I'm getting gray hair?

Low vitamin B12 is the most commonly reported, often along with low folate and biotin. A blood test is the only way to know.

Which form of B12 is best for gray hair?

No study has compared B12 forms for gray hair. Methylcobalamin and cyanocobalamin are both widely used to raise B12 levels; your doctor can advise if you're deficient.

Can low B12 cause gray hair at a young age?

Low B12 is found more often in people who gray young, but genetics is usually the main reason. Testing is worth it if you're graying in your teens or 20s.
