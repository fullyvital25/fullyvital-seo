Rosemary oil is one of the few natural oils with a human trial behind it for hair growth. In that trial, people with pattern hair loss who used rosemary oil on the scalp for six months saw a similar increase in hair count to people using minoxidil 2%, with less scalp itching. It's promising, but the evidence is still thin, and it takes months, not weeks.

This guide covers what rosemary oil does for hair, what the research actually shows, how to use it safely, how long it takes, and how it compares with other oils.

Key fact: In the main trial, neither rosemary oil nor minoxidil made a measurable difference at three months. Both did at six. Give any scalp routine at least that long before you judge it.

The Short Answer

  • One randomized trial found rosemary oil increased hair count over six months about as much as minoxidil 2%.
  • Lab and animal research suggests rosemary may work partly by blocking 5α-reductase, the enzyme that turns testosterone into DHT.
  • Results take around six months. Nothing changed at three months in the trial.
  • Always dilute rosemary essential oil in a carrier oil, and patch-test first. Undiluted essential oils can irritate the scalp.

What Does Rosemary Oil Do for Your Hair?

Rosemary oil is thought to support hair growth in three ways: it may improve blood flow to the scalp, it may reduce the effect of DHT on hair follicles, and it has antioxidant and anti-inflammatory compounds such as carnosic acid and rosmarinic acid. Most of that comes from lab and animal work. The human evidence so far is one main trial.

It also leaves hair smelling fresh and can make the scalp feel cleaner, which is part of why it's so popular in hair oils and serums.

Does Rosemary Oil Actually Help Hair Growth?

There's reasonable early evidence that it can, in people with common pattern hair loss. A six-month randomized trial found that rosemary oil and minoxidil 2% both increased hair count, with no significant difference between them. That's encouraging, but it's one trial of 100 people, and it hasn't been repeated at a larger scale.

So rosemary oil is best seen as a gentle, low-risk option to try, not a guaranteed fix. If your hair loss is sudden, patchy or getting worse quickly, see a doctor or dermatologist first. How to stop hair loss: what actually works.

How Long Does Rosemary Oil Take to Work?

Expect around six months. In the trial, hair count hadn't changed meaningfully at three months in either group, and the increase showed up at six months. That fits how hair grows: each strand grows about a centimeter a month, and follicles take time to shift back into their growth phase.

How to Use Rosemary Oil on Your Hair

  1. Dilute it. Mix a few drops of rosemary essential oil into a carrier oil such as jojoba, argan or coconut oil. Never put undiluted essential oil straight onto your scalp.
  2. Patch-test first. Put a little of the mix on your inner arm and wait a day to check for redness or itching.
  3. Massage it into the scalp, not just the lengths. Your fingertips are enough. Why scalp massage helps.
  4. Leave it on for at least a few hours or overnight, then shampoo it out.
  5. Be consistent. A few times a week for six months tells you more than a daily burst for a fortnight.

Can You Make Your Own Rosemary Hair Oil?

Yes. The simplest version is a carrier oil with a few drops of rosemary essential oil, kept in a dark glass bottle. Some people steep fresh rosemary sprigs in warm oil instead, which gives a much milder oil. Rosemary water, made by simmering rosemary in water, is another option, though there's little research on it.

Who Should Be Careful With Rosemary Oil?

Most people tolerate diluted rosemary oil well. Stop if your scalp gets red, itchy or sore. If you're pregnant or breastfeeding, have a sensitive scalp or eczema, or take medication, ask your doctor or pharmacist before using essential oils.

Rosemary has the strongest human evidence of the popular hair oils. Others are mostly backed by animal studies or tradition, and work better as conditioners than as growth support.

What the Research Shows

A randomized trial (Panahi et al., 2015) compared rosemary oil with minoxidil 2% in 100 people with androgenetic alopecia (common pattern hair loss), 50 in each group, for six months. Neither group showed a significant change in hair count at three months. At six months, both groups had a significant increase compared with the start, with no significant difference between them. Scalp itching rose in both groups but was more common with minoxidil.

A lab and animal study (Murata et al., 2013) found that rosemary leaf extract applied to the skin improved hair regrowth in mice whose regrowth had been held back by testosterone. In lab tests, the extract blocked 82.4% to 94.6% of 5α-reductase activity, the enzyme that makes DHT. That helps explain how rosemary might work, but results in mice don't always carry over to people.

A 2025 review of non-prescription options for pattern hair loss (Gupta et al.) looked at 13 investigator-blinded, controlled trials of products including rosemary oil, caffeine, pumpkin seed oil and saw palmetto. These showed improvements in one or more measures, such as hair density or hair thickness, with few side effects. The authors call for larger, higher-quality trials.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can rosemary oil make my hair grow faster?

It may help more hair grow over time, but it won't change how fast each strand grows, which is about a centimeter a month. In the main trial, the increase in hair count took six months to show.

How do I apply rosemary oil to my hair?

Mix a few drops of rosemary essential oil into a carrier oil, massage it into your scalp, leave it on for a few hours or overnight, then wash it out. Patch-test first, and never use it undiluted.

Is rosemary oil as good as minoxidil?

In one six-month trial, rosemary oil and minoxidil 2% increased hair count by a similar amount, and rosemary caused less scalp itching. It's a single trial, so minoxidil still has far more evidence behind it.

Can rosemary essential oil help thinning hair?

It may help with common pattern thinning, based on early evidence. If thinning is sudden or patchy, see a doctor to find the cause first.

Is rosemary good for the skin?

Diluted rosemary oil is used in skin care for its antioxidant compounds, but undiluted essential oil can irritate skin. Patch-test any new product.

How often should I use rosemary oil on my hair?

A few times a week is a common routine. Being consistent for several months matters more than using it every day.
