A receding hairline is usually the first sign of pattern hair loss in men, and is mostly down to genes and DHT. In women the hairline usually holds, and thinning shows as a widening part instead, but a receding hairline in women can also come from tight hairstyles or, less often, a scarring condition. A maturing hairline, which moves back slightly in the late teens and 20s, is normal and isn't the same as balding.

This guide covers how to tell a mature hairline from a receding one, the causes in men and women, what can slow it, and when to see a doctor.

Key fact: Pattern hair loss keeps progressing slowly with age. Treatments work best on hair that's thinning, not on skin that's already been bald for years, so acting early matters most.

The Short Answer

  • A mature hairline moves back evenly by about a finger's width. A receding hairline keeps going, often deeper at the temples.
  • In men, the cause is usually pattern hair loss (androgenetic alopecia). The Norwood scale tracks how far it has gone.
  • In women, a receding hairline is more often from tight hairstyles (traction alopecia) or, rarely, frontal fibrosing alopecia.
  • Minoxidil, finasteride (for men, on prescription) and low-level laser have the best evidence for slowing pattern loss.

What Causes a Receding Hairline?

In men

Almost always pattern hair loss. Follicles at the temples and crown are sensitive to DHT, a hormone made from testosterone, and gradually shrink, producing finer hairs until they stop. Genes decide how sensitive your follicles are.

In women

Female pattern hair loss usually spares the hairline and thins the top of the head, so a receding front hairline points to other causes. The most common is traction alopecia, from years of tight ponytails, braids, buns or extensions. A rarer cause is frontal fibrosing alopecia, a scarring condition that mostly affects women after menopause and needs a dermatologist. Female pattern hair loss and normal part vs. thinning.

Other causes

Temporary shedding after stress, illness or childbirth thins hair all over, including at the front, and usually recovers. Sudden hair shedding.

How to Slow a Receding Hairline

  • Start early. The best results come while there are still thinning hairs to work with.
  • Minoxidil (over the counter) helps follicles stay in their growth phase. Minoxidil explained.
  • Finasteride (prescription, for men) lowers DHT. Talk it through with your doctor. Finasteride explained.
  • Low-level laser therapy has controlled trials behind it. Low-level laser therapy.
  • Microneedling alongside minoxidil has improved results in studies. Microneedling for the hairline.
  • Ease the tension if you wear tight styles. Traction alopecia can recover if caught early.
  • Cover the basics: enough protein, iron, vitamin D and zinc, and a healthy scalp. Vitamins for hair growth.

Can a Receding Hairline Grow Back?

Sometimes, partly. Thinning hairs at the front can thicken with treatment, and traction alopecia caught early often recovers. Areas that have been smooth and bald for years rarely regrow with any treatment; a hair transplant is the main option there. Natural options such as rosemary oil have early evidence for pattern loss, but less than minoxidil. Rosemary oil for hair growth.

When to See a Doctor

  • Your hairline is receding quickly, or you're a woman with a receding front hairline
  • The skin along the hairline looks shiny, red or scaly, or your eyebrows are thinning too
  • You have patchy loss, or hair loss with other symptoms

What the Research Shows

A classification study of male pattern baldness (Norwood, 1975) set out the stages now known as the Norwood scale and used it to measure how common pattern baldness was at different ages in 1,000 white adult men. It found that baldness kept progressing with age, even in later years.

A 2024 review of hair loss for family doctors (Dakkak et al.) groups non-scarring hair loss into diffuse, patterned and focal types. It describes pattern hair loss as the most common form, usually genetic, with minoxidil as first-line treatment and oral finasteride as an option for men. Traction alopecia is treated by reducing tension on the hair.

A systematic review and meta-analysis of randomized trials (Adil and Godwin, 2017) found that low-level laser therapy, 5% and 2% minoxidil and 1 mg finasteride were all better than placebo at improving hair growth in men with pattern hair loss, and 2% minoxidil was better than placebo in women.

Frequently Asked Questions

How can I slow a receding hairline?

Start early. Minoxidil, finasteride (for men, on prescription) and low-level laser have the most evidence. Avoid tight hairstyles, and correct any deficiencies.

Can finasteride regrow the hairline?

Finasteride mainly slows or stops further loss, and some men see regrowth, more often at the crown than the front. Results take months. Discuss it with your doctor.

What are some home remedies for thinning hair at the front?

Gentle scalp care, looser hairstyles, a diet with enough protein and iron, and rosemary oil, which has early evidence. Over-the-counter minoxidil is the best-proven option you can buy without a prescription.

What is frontal fibrosing alopecia?

A scarring type of hair loss that slowly moves the front hairline back, often with eyebrow loss. It mainly affects women after menopause. See a dermatologist, because early treatment matters.

Is a receding hairline at 20 normal?

Some movement is normal as the hairline matures. If it keeps going, deepens at the temples or the hair gets finer, it may be early pattern loss.
