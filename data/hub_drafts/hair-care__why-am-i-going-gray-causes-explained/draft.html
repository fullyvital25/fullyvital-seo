Going gray early usually comes down to your genes, but it isn't only genetic. Low vitamin B12 or iron stores, oxidative stress, chronic stress and smoking are all linked to graying sooner than expected, and some of these you can change.

This guide explains what counts as premature graying, what causes it, when it's worth seeing a doctor, and what you can do at each age.

Key fact: Premature graying runs on the same biology as ordinary graying: pigment cells in the follicle slow down. It just happens earlier, and that means the modifiable factors matter more.

What Counts as Premature Graying?

Premature graying means going gray much earlier than is typical for your background. The usual medical definition is graying before age 20 in Caucasian people and before age 30 in African American people, as set out in a 2018 review (Kumar et al.).

Graying in your 30s is earlier than average for many people, but it isn't "premature" by that definition. It's still worth looking at the causes below. For how graying usually progresses with age, see what causes gray hair.

Why Do Some People Go Gray Early?

Hair turns gray when melanocytes, the pigment-making cells in each follicle, make less melanin. In premature graying, several things can push that process forward at once.

Genetics

Genes play a large part. If a parent went gray young, you're more likely to as well.

Low Vitamin B12 and Iron Stores

Making melanin depends on nutrients. Studies of young people with premature graying have found lower vitamin B12 and lower iron stores than in people without it. Gray hair and nutrient depletion and which vitamins matter.

Oxidative Stress

Producing pigment generates reactive molecules, which the follicle's antioxidants normally neutralize. When oxidative stress outpaces those defenses, pigment production suffers. More on oxidative stress and hydrogen peroxide.

Chronic Stress

Long-term stress is linked to graying, acting at the root over weeks and months rather than overnight. Can stress cause gray hair?

Smoking and Lifestyle

Smoking is linked to graying earlier. A sedentary lifestyle and irregular eating have also been reported more often in people who gray young. Smoking and gray hair.

Going Gray Early, by Age

  • Teens and 20s: graying this early is more likely to be premature, so a check-up for nutrient levels and thyroid function is sensible. Going gray in your 20s.
  • 30s: often genetics plus lifestyle factors such as stress, diet and smoking. Going gray in your 30s.
  • 40s and 50s: mostly normal age-related graying, sometimes alongside hormonal change. Going gray in your 40s and 50s and perimenopause and gray hair.

Is Early Graying a Sign of a Health Problem?

Usually not. Most premature graying is genetic. But early graying has been associated with some premature aging disorders, atopy (allergic conditions such as eczema) and autoimmune conditions. The 2018 review recommends that people who gray prematurely be checked for related syndromes and metabolic disease.

See a doctor if your graying is sudden or patchy, started in childhood or your teens, or comes with fatigue, skin changes or other symptoms. When sudden gray hair is worth a doctor's visit.

What the Research Shows

In a study of 37 young Indian adults with premature graying and 37 matched controls (Chakrabarty et al., 2016), people who grayed early had lower serum ferritin (iron stores), lower vitamin B12 and lower HDL cholesterol. They were also more likely to report a sedentary lifestyle and irregular eating habits.

A 2020 study of 50 people under 25 with premature graying and 30 controls (Saxena et al.) found higher levels of an oxidative damage marker (malondialdehyde) and lower levels of the antioxidants glutathione and superoxide dismutase in those who grayed early. The more gray hair someone had, the greater the imbalance.

The 2018 review by Kumar and colleagues notes that the exact cause of premature graying is still unknown, and that hair dye remains the main cosmetic option alongside nutritional supplementation.

How Does Age-Related Graying Differ from Premature Graying?

Age-related graying is gradual and driven mainly by the slow decline of the follicle's pigment stem cells over decades. Premature graying follows the same biology on a faster timeline. Because genetics and modifiable factors such as nutrient gaps, oxidative stress, stress and smoking all play a part, early graying isn't always a fixed fate. Why acting early matters.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does early white hair mean?

Usually it means you've inherited a tendency to gray early. It can also reflect low B12 or iron stores, oxidative stress, smoking or stress, and occasionally an underlying condition, so very early graying is worth a check-up.

Why is my hair turning white at 16?

Graying in the teens is premature by any definition. Genetics is the most common reason, but a doctor can check for nutrient deficiencies, thyroid problems and related conditions.

What vitamin are you lacking if your hair is turning gray?

Low vitamin B12 and low iron stores (ferritin) have been found more often in people who gray early. Only treat a deficiency that a blood test confirms.

Can premature graying be reversed?

Sometimes, partly. Correcting a confirmed deficiency or treating an underlying condition can help in some cases, but no product can guarantee full color restoration, and results vary.

How do I stop getting white hair?

You can't stop genetic graying, but you can address what's linked to graying early: correct confirmed deficiencies, don't smoke, and manage stress.

Why am I going gray in my 30s?

Most often it's genetics, sometimes sped up by stress, smoking, diet or nutrient gaps. By the medical definition it isn't usually "premature", but it's earlier than average for many people.
