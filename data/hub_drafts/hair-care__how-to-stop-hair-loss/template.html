How you stop hair loss depends on what's causing it. Temporary shedding after stress, illness, childbirth or rapid weight loss usually settles on its own once the trigger passes. Pattern hair loss, the most common kind, is gradual and ongoing. Minoxidil, finasteride and low-level laser have the best evidence for it, and they work best when started early. A doctor or dermatologist can tell you which you have.

This guide explains how to work out what kind of hair loss you have, what the research says works, what you can do without drugs, and when to see a doctor.

Key fact: Most people lose 50 to 100 hairs a day, which is normal. Shedding that's suddenly heavier, a widening part, a receding hairline or bald patches are the signs worth acting on.

The Short Answer

  • Find the cause first. Shedding, pattern thinning and patchy loss have different causes and different fixes.
  • Temporary shedding (telogen effluvium) usually recovers by itself within months once the trigger is gone.
  • For pattern hair loss, minoxidil, finasteride (for men, on prescription) and low-level laser have the strongest evidence.
  • Without drugs, you can correct deficiencies, avoid crash diets, manage stress, treat your scalp gently and try evidence-backed natural options.

Step 2: Fix What You Can

  • Get a blood test if you're shedding or thinning: low iron stores, vitamin D, B12 and thyroid problems are common, treatable contributors. The vitamins that matter for hair.
  • Eat enough protein and calories. Crash diets are a classic trigger of shedding. Protein-rich foods for hair growth.
  • Manage stress and sleep. Major stress can push many hairs into the shedding phase at once. Cortisol and hair loss.
  • Treat your hair gently. Avoid tight hairstyles, harsh chemical treatments and too much heat.
  • Look after your scalp. Treat dandruff and itching, which can make shedding worse.

Step 3: Options for Pattern Hair Loss

Pattern hair loss is driven by genes and by DHT, a hormone made from testosterone that gradually shrinks sensitive follicles. These are the options with the most evidence:

  • Minoxidil (over the counter, for men and women) helps follicles stay in their growth phase. It takes several months to show results, and the benefit lasts only while you keep using it. Minoxidil explained.
  • Finasteride (prescription, for men) lowers DHT. It isn't suitable for women who are or may become pregnant. Talk it through with your doctor. Finasteride explained.
  • Low-level laser therapy (caps, combs and helmets) has controlled trials behind it. Low-level laser therapy.
  • Microneedling, combined with minoxidil, has improved results in clinical studies. Microneedling for hair loss.

Women may also be offered other prescription options. A dermatologist can advise on what suits you.

Can You Reverse Hair Loss Without Drugs?

Sometimes. Shedding caused by a deficiency, a crash diet, stress or childbirth often recovers once the cause is dealt with. Pattern hair loss is harder: drug-free options can help, but the evidence for them is weaker than for minoxidil and finasteride. The best-supported natural options include rosemary oil, which matched minoxidil 2% in one trial, and pumpkin seed oil, which beat placebo in a small trial in men. When to See a Doctor

  • Bald patches, or hair coming out in clumps
  • A painful, red, scaly or itchy scalp
  • Hair loss along with other symptoms, such as tiredness, weight change or irregular periods
  • Rapid loss at a young age, or loss after starting a new medication

What the Research Shows

A systematic review and meta-analysis of randomized controlled trials (Adil and Godwin, 2017) found that low-level laser therapy, 5% and 2% minoxidil and 1 mg finasteride were all better than placebo at improving hair growth in men with androgenetic alopecia, and that 2% minoxidil was better than placebo in women. The authors note high variation between studies.

A review of telogen effluvium (Chien Yin et al., 2021) describes it as a common cause of diffuse hair loss, usually set off by physical stress such as childbirth or sudden weight loss. It has a high rate of remission, but its mechanism isn't fully understood and there's no targeted treatment.

A randomized, evaluator-blinded pilot study (Dhurat et al., 2013) gave 100 men with mild to moderate androgenetic alopecia either 5% minoxidil alone or minoxidil plus weekly in-clinic microneedling. After 12 weeks, the average gain in hair count was 91.4 hairs per square centimeter with microneedling, against 22.2 with minoxidil alone.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I reverse hair loss without using drugs?

Often, if it's temporary shedding from stress, illness, a deficiency or a crash diet. Pattern hair loss is harder to reverse without drugs, though some natural options such as rosemary oil have early evidence.

What shampoo does a dermatologist recommend for hair loss?

Dermatologists sometimes suggest ketoconazole, an anti-dandruff shampoo, for pattern hair loss, though the evidence is limited. Otherwise, a gentle shampoo that keeps your scalp clean and free of flaking is enough. Shampoos for thinning hair.

Can you make your hair thicker?

You can make it look and feel thicker with the right cut and products, and support thicker growth over time by correcting deficiencies, protecting your scalp and treating pattern hair loss early.

Does all hair thinning lead to baldness?

No. Temporary shedding usually recovers fully. Pattern hair loss tends to progress slowly, but how far varies a lot, and treatment can slow it.

Does a red light cap work for hair loss?

Low-level laser and red light devices have controlled trials showing better hair growth than sham devices in pattern hair loss. Results take months, and devices vary.

What is the evidence for hair oil regrowth?

Rosemary oil has one trial showing results similar to minoxidil 2% after six months. Most other oils have only animal studies or none, so treat them as conditioners rather than growth treatments.
