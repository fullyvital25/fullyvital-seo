These grooming practices are also frequently associated with traction alopecia in clinical studies. However, there is little to no quantitative scientific data on the surface and internal hair fibre damage caused by grooming, including combing, on Afro hair.</AbstractText><AbstractText Label="METHODS" NlmCategory="METHODS">Qualitative and quantitative techniques such as scanning electron microscopy, cuticle cohesion and tensile testing were used to determine the effects of frequent braiding on the integrity of female Afro hair in 15 frequent braiders and 15 occasional braiders (control group) in Johannesburg, South Africa. Frequent braiders were women who braided more than 8 times per year, while occasional braiders typically limit braiding to only twice 2 per year. The severity of traction alopecia was quantified in both groups using the standard Marginal Traction Alopecia Scoring system. Surface damage on naturally curly Afro hair, that was combed but not braided, was assessed as another hairstyle option.</AbstractText><AbstractText Label="RESULTS" NlmCategory="RESULTS">All measurements showed a relationship between surface and internal hair fibre damage and braiding frequency. Frequent braiders had thinner hair, with more cuticle damage as shown by SEM and cuticle cohesion assessments. Their hair fibres were also significantly weaker (p&#xa0;&lt;&#xa0;0.05), as shown by the tensile testing. In addition, they also had higher traction alopecia severity scores. In the combing impact assessment, a linear trend was observed between surface hair fibre damage and the number of combing strokes. Cuticle damage from 480 combing strokes was more severe than that from frequent braiding.</AbstractText><AbstractText Label="CONCLUSION" NlmCategory="CONCLUSIONS">Frequent braiding and combing damage natural Afro hair. Quantification of damage caused by different grooming practices identifies a need to develop products that could help mitigate specific hair damage associated with grooming practices. The degree of damage and subsequent hair loss could also be minimized by education on best braiding practices to protect the hair fibre and avoid hair follicle miniaturization caused by high traction La gravit&#xe9; de l'alop&#xe9;cie de traction a &#xe9;t&#xe9; &#xe9;galement quantifi&#xe9;e dans les deux groupes &#xe0; l'aide du syst&#xe8;me standard de notation de l'alop&#xe9;cie tractionnelle. Parall&#xe8;lement, &#xe0; titre de comparaison, les dommages de surface ont &#xe9;t&#xe9; &#xe9;galement &#xe9;valu&#xe9;s sur des cheveux d&#x2019;origine Africaine naturellement boucl&#xe9;s, non tress&#xe9;s, mais qui ont subi un peignage r&#xe9;p&#xe9;t&#xe9;. R&#xc9;SULTATS: Toutes les mesures ont montr&#xe9; une relation entre les dommages de surface et internes de la fibre et la fr&#xe9;quence de tressage. Les tresseurs fr&#xe9;quents ont des cheveux plus fins, avec un endommagement plus important de la cuticule, comme le montrent les &#xe9;valuations MEB et la coh&#xe9;sion cuticulaire. Les cheveux sont &#xe9;galement significativement plus fragiles (p &lt;0,05), comme le montrent les tests de traction. De plus, ces femmes ont &#xe9;galement des scores d'alop&#xe9;cie de traction plus &#xe9;lev&#xe9;s. Dans le cas sp&#xe9;cifique du peignage r&#xe9;p&#xe9;t&#xe9;, une tendance lin&#xe9;aire a &#xe9;t&#xe9; observ&#xe9;e entre les dommages en surface de la fibre capillaire et le nombre de coups de peigne.
