Humans may be naturally exposed to boron through food and drinking water, or via anthropogenic sources such as consumer products. The World Health Organisation established an acceptable safe range of population mean intakes for boron of 1-13&#x2009;mg/day. Most studies of dietary boron intake show a range of 1-2&#x2009;mg/day. Consumer products have been estimated to contribute a geometric mean daily intake of 0.1&#x2009;mg to total boron exposure; however, there are few published surveys of consumer exposure to boron from use of cleaning products. The Government of Canada published a draft screening assessment report of boric acid, its salts and precursors that included estimates of consumer exposure to boron found as ingredients in consumer products. The manufacturers of consumer cleaning products conducted a survey of boron content of current products and estimated exposure using the publicly available exposure tool ConsExpo Web. Dermal exposures to boron during cleaning product use were estimated to result in annual internal exposures ranging from &#x226a;0.001 to 0.36&#x2009;&#x3bc;g/kg bw/day based on dermal absorption of 0.5%. Using a conservative point of departure for hazard assessment (2,900&#x2009;&#x3bc;g boron/kg bw/day), estimated margins of exposure for dermal exposures to boron from cleaning product use range from 8,056 to &gt;1,000,000. This work demonstrates that exposure to boron from cleaning product use is very low and essentially insignificant when compared to other (e.g. dietary) sources of boron intake by Canadian
