This disorder may have significant psychological impact and lead to anxiety and depression.</AbstractText><AbstractText Label="OBJECTIVE" NlmCategory="UNASSIGNED">In a single center, double blind, randomized, placebo-controlled study, the effects of oral Pycnogenol&#xae; intake (3&#x2009;&#xd7;&#x2009;50&#x2009;mg/day for a total of 6 months) on hair density, scalp microcirculation, and a variety of skin physiological parameters was studied in Han Chinese menopausal women (<i>N</i>&#x2009;=&#x2009;76) in Shanghai, China.</AbstractText><AbstractText Label="METHODS" NlmCategory="UNASSIGNED">Measurements were taken at the beginning and after 2&#xa0;and 6&#xa0;months, respectively. Changes in microcirculation were detected as resting flux on the scalp by reflection photoplethysmography.</AbstractText><AbstractText Label="RESULTS" NlmCategory="UNASSIGNED">Pycnogenol&#xae; intake significantly increased hair density by 30% and 23% after 2&#xa0;and 6&#xa0;months of treatment, respectively, as detected by Trichoscan&#xae; evaluation of digital photographs. Interestingly, photoplethysmography revealed that this beneficial effect was associated with a decrease in resting flux of the scalp skin, which might indicate an improvement of microcirculation. None of these effects were observed in the placebo taking group. In addition, a significant transient decrease of transepidermal water loss was observed in scalp skin under Pycnogenol,&#xae;&#xa0;but not placebo treatment.</AbstractText><AbstractText Label="CONCLUSION" NlmCategory="UNASSIGNED">Oral intake of Pycnogenol&#xae; might have the potential to reduce hair loss in postmenopausal women.</AbstractText><CopyrightInformation>&#xa9; 2023 The Authors.
